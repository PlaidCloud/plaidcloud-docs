## Added

- **Data Foundation brings your ERP data together in one Common Finance Model.** The Common Finance Model brings together ERP data from multiple ERP systems and instances into a single model: one chart of accounts, one set of entities and one reporting currency. Reports, metrics and AI assistants built on it all read the same numbers, whichever ERP they came from.

  - **Direct from your ERP.** General ledger, trial balance, master data and budgets arrive through PlaidCloud's direct ERP connections. An **Entity crosswalk** maps each source's company codes onto the same entities, and files fill in anything a system doesn't hold, such as currency rates or allocation drivers.
  - **One model, one currency.** **Load** conforms every source into the Common Finance Model and translates it into your reporting currency: profit and loss at the period's average rate, the balance sheet at the closing rate, and equity at the historical rate. Hierarchies are built from your master data.
  - **Check readiness.** Checks run for each entity and period: the trial balance and sales tie to the general ledger, every product, customer and account is known, currencies translate, and every entity is mapped.
  - **Keep it current.** **Refresh nightly** reloads the model every night, and a missing or empty file finishes with a warning, not an error.

  Start a project from a template, such as **Enterprise Profitability**, and the project's **Foundation** tab takes you from connected ERPs to a loaded, checked model. See [Data Foundation: The Common Finance Model](/guides/projects/data-foundation/).

New projects then store their tables and run their workflows in your warehouse, and removing the warehouse revokes PlaidCloud's access there. See [Use Your Own Warehouse](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/).

  - **Sign in with Snowflake or Databricks.** Instead of pasting a credential, an administrator can attach the warehouse by signing in. See [Sign In With Snowflake or Databricks](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#sign-in-with-snowflake-or-databricks).
  - **Connect more than one.** **Make default** in **Manage warehouses** sends new projects to another warehouse, while existing projects stay where they are. See [Manage Warehouses](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#manage-warehouses).
  - **See what a run costs.** Run history shows a **Warehouse cost** column in credits or DBUs for each run. See [Warehouse Cost per Run](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#warehouse-cost-per-run).
  - **Your governance stays in place.** Descriptions are written to the warehouse's own catalog, and Snowflake row access and masking policies and Databricks row filters and column masks stay attached when PlaidCloud replaces, recreates or clones a table. See [Your Policies and Descriptions Carry Over](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#your-policies-and-descriptions-carry-over).

- **Agree once how a business number is calculated, and every AI answer uses that calculation.** A project Architect defines a **metric**, such as *Net Revenue* or *Headcount*: its source table, how its rows add up, the rows that always count, the lookup tables it joins, and the columns it can be broken down by. A definition is live as soon as it's saved, and a save that names a missing table or column, or a lookup with more than one row per key, is refused. Ask an AI assistant for the metric by name or synonym and the figures are calculated by the definition, under your own row access, not worked out by the assistant. Definitions are managed through an AI assistant or the REST API. See [Metric Definitions](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/).

  - **Derived metrics.** Define a ratio, sum or difference of two metrics, such as net revenue per FTE, or a metric times a fixed factor. See [Derive a Metric From Others](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#derive-a-metric-from-others).
  - **Compare, rank and share.** Ask for the variance against budget, the top or bottom N with ties kept, or each row's share of the total. See [Compare, Rank and Share](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#compare-rank-and-share).
  - **Your words, the agreed values.** Bind a breakdown column to a dimension, and a word such as *EMEA* becomes every region beneath it. A word that matches nothing, or more than one member, comes back as candidates to ask you about, never a guess. See [Ask About a Metric](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#ask-about-a-metric).
  - **Fiscal time.** Tell a metric where its rows sit in your fiscal calendar, and ask for a fiscal period, year to date, year over year or period over period. See [Compare Over Time](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#compare-over-time).

  PlaidCloud's own AI assistant answers a business number from a metric first, and shows the figures in their own card marked with a ✓, which never attaches to the assistant's own text. See [Business Numbers](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#business-numbers) and [Figures From a Metric](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#figures-from-a-metric).

- **Ask Weave whether you're ready to close, and check that two tables tie out.** No AI is involved in either.

  - **Close readiness.** Name the workflows a close, a forecast or any deadline depends on as a **readiness set**, from **Readiness** on a project's **Weave** tab. One checklist answers for all of them: "11 of 13 ready; 1 not ready; 1 couldn't check." A workflow is ready only when every check Weave could run on it passed, and anything Weave couldn't judge shows as **Couldn't check** with the reason, never as ready. A last run with too little earlier history to compare it with, such as a new workflow's, counts as usual. A step that writes no table, such as one that sends an email or exports a file, has no rows or empty values to check, so it doesn't hold a workflow at **Couldn't check**. See [Check Readiness Before a Close](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-readiness/).
  - **Reconciliations.** Compare two tables, or two periods of one table, row by row on the key columns you pair. Weave says whether they tie out, how far apart they are and which rows differ, and never says "ties out" for data it couldn't read in full. A second Architect approves each reconciliation before it's used. See [Check That Two Tables Tie Out](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-reconciliation/).

- **Weave watches more of what can go wrong, and tells you between runs.** Weave's checks use no AI. Every finding, and every answer from the AI assistant, has a **How Weave knows** panel: for a finding, the check and the values behind it; for an answer, each query the assistant ran, with every sentence marked **Matched**, **Unmatched** or **Not checked**. See [What Weave Checks Today](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#what-weave-checks-today), [How Weave Knows](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#how-weave-knows) and [How Weave Knows in the AI Assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#how-weave-knows).

  - **Tables written on time.** Weave reports a table that hasn't been written since its schedule came due, or for longer than a freshness threshold you set under **Freshness** in the table's details, from 1 hour to 180 days.
  - **Inputs that are stale or broken.** Weave reports a workflow that reads a table another workflow has written since, and every workflow and dashboard that rests on a failed run, including a dashboard whose charts are built on SQL datasets. **Sync Dependencies** adds the connectors missing between a step and the table it reads. See [Sync Dependencies](/guides/workflows/sync-dependencies/).
  - **Data that changed shape.** Weave reports a decimal column whose values spread very differently after a run, a join that starts writing rows more than once, values an Extract step left empty because they couldn't be converted to their column's type, and an import that found no files. It also reports a metric whose latest period is far from usual, naming the item that drove it. Weave allows for month-end and weekday patterns, so a load that's always heavy at month end isn't reported. See [Unusual Metric Totals](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-metric-insights/).
  - **Runs that got slower.** Weave names the step that made a run slow, and reports a workflow whose newer runs take at least a quarter longer than its older ones, naming the steps whose time grew most.
  - **Told between runs.** An hourly check tells each problem's owner once, in an in-app message, and a **Weave** button in the header counts the problems you haven't read across all your projects. See [Being Told Between Runs](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#being-told-between-runs) and [The Weave Button in the Header](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#the-weave-button-in-the-header).
  - **Owners, comments and expected warnings.** Each finding has an owner, by default whoever last ran its workflow. **Assign** hands it on, comments record progress, and the **Team** view shows who owns what. An Architect can mark a warning **This Is Expected**, with a reason and an end date. See [Give a Finding an Owner](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#give-a-finding-an-owner) and [Mark a Finding as Expected](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#mark-a-finding-as-expected).

- **The fix assistant explains an error, proposes a change you approve on a card, re-runs the step and can undo the fix.** For the most common failures, such as a column that isn't in the table, an empty required setting, SQL the database can't read or a value of the wrong type, the first reply explains the usual cause and how to fix it. A proposed change appears as a card that states why the step failed and shows each changed setting with its current value in red and its new value in green. **Approve** applies it, and the assistant then offers to re-run the step and diagnoses a new failure. **Fix** on a finding in the **Weave** tab opens the assistant on that problem. **Undo** restores the step's earlier settings, with **Undo & Run** to rebuild its output. See [Fixing Workflow Errors with AI](/guides/ai-assistant/fixing-workflow-errors/), [Fix a Finding with the AI Assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#fix-a-finding-with-the-ai-assistant) and [Undo a Fix](/guides/ai-assistant/fixing-workflow-errors/#undo-a-fix).

- **See everything Weave did in a project, and export it for your auditors.** **Activity** on a project's **Weave** tab lists the problems Weave found and later stopped finding, every change the AI assistant proposed, who requested and who approved or rejected it, exactly what it replaced and wrote, and how it ended. Weave records your approval before it makes a change, and makes no change it can't record. **Export CSV** or **Export PDF** saves a period as audit evidence, and entries can't be edited or deleted. **Summary** shows a month at a glance: problems found and resolved, approved changes and how many were undone. ## Changed

- **One Weave tab holds your findings and your Weave AI chats.** Chats are tabs inside the **Weave** tab, after **Findings**. Each finding is a card with its own **Fix**, **Go To** and **Mark Read** buttons, a dashed card marks something Weave couldn't check, and chips filter the list by what needs attention. A finding about an expression names where the error is, and the expression itself is under **How Weave knows**. Weave mentions each problem once when runs end, and messages about one project join into one rather than stacking up. The tab opens quickly in large projects because Weave checks again only what has changed. The Weave column is gone from the workflow list. See [The Weave Tab](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/) and [Being Told When a Run Ends](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#being-told-when-a-run-ends).

- **Ctrl/Cmd-K opens the command palette from any screen.** Before you type, it lists what you opened last and the actions you can take: go to Home, Projects, Dashboards or Documents, switch between light and dark, connect your AI, or get help. Typing narrows both lists, and from two characters on it also searches projects, workflows, tables, dashboards and Panel apps. See [Search and Commands](/guides/launcher/#search-and-commands).

- **Steps catch problems when you save, not when they run.** Saving a step whose settings would fail its run, or give the wrong result, is refused with a message saying what to change. Each step's reference page lists what it checks. A mapped source column that the step or its source table no longer has is flagged in red, and saving opens a dialog with **Remove All and Save**, **Save Anyway** or **Cancel**. Join, lookup and spatial steps keep such rows in place instead of deleting them on their own. An expression that uses an undefined variable names the variable, and a step refused when it runs explains why in plain words. See [Source Columns That Are No Longer Listed](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-extract/#source-columns-that-are-no-longer-listed), [Columns Missing From the Source Table](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-extract/#columns-missing-from-the-source-table) and [Managing Step Errors](/guides/workflows/managing-step-errors/).

- **A missing or empty source is never an error.** A file import whose file is missing or empty finishes with a warning naming the path and leaves its target table empty with its declared columns, so later steps read zero rows. Document steps over a missing or empty folder report "Nothing to process" and the workflow continues. Remote file steps warn on a missing path and refuse an unsafe one. Checks for a missing file work on OneDrive, SharePoint and SFTP. **Report: Generate Batch** with an empty batch table finishes with a warning and produces no reports. See [Import Steps](/reference/workflow-steps/import/), [Document Workflow Steps](/reference/workflow-steps/document/) and [Document Path Conditions](/guides/workflows/step-conditions/#document-path-conditions).

- **Large workflows run reliably, and Stop cancels imports and exports.** When PlaidCloud is busy, steps wait for capacity instead of failing, and capacity is added as steps queue up. Import External Database Tables runs up to 10 tables at a time. If the server running a step is lost, a step that replaces its output runs again automatically, and one that can't be repeated safely fails once with an *interrupted* message. **Stop** cancels running import and export jobs, and a stopped run reports Stopped. See [Interrupted Steps](/guides/workflows/run-a-workflow/#interrupted-steps) and [Pause, Stop, and Resume](/guides/workflows/run-a-workflow/#pause-stop-and-resume).

- **Every Lakehouse stores and calculates the same way.** An empty field from Import CSV, Import XML or Import Table Archive loads as a null, so a later step that tests for `= ''` or `= 0` needs to test for null instead. `cast(x, Float)` is 64-bit everywhere, so large amounts keep their digits, and Float columns PlaidCloud creates are double precision. JSON columns, **Table: Delete Rows** and conditions that aggregate work on every Lakehouse. Steps that build their own results, such as Solver, Forecast, and Pivot and Allocation Split when they run in pandas, load them as typed data, much faster. Table Explorer shows the minimum, maximum, mean, sum, standard deviation and variance of a numeric column. See [Casting to Float](/reference/workflow-steps/common/advanced-data-mapper-usage/#casting-to-float) and [Column Data and Unique Counts](/guides/data/table-explorer/#column-data-and-unique-counts).

- **File exports keep their types.** Parquet files keep whole-number columns as integers, store timestamps in microseconds and label a time zone as UTC. Avro files keep dates, timestamps and booleans typed, and a single-table Avro export is named after the export. Choosing many files for a Parquet export stops with a message that Parquet writes a single file. Very large Parquet, ORC and JSON exports are written as the rows are read, so they no longer run out of memory, and Parquet uses the compression you choose. A PlaidLink agent on version 1.2.33 or later receives an exported table as a Parquet file. See [Export to Parquet](/reference/workflow-steps/export/export-to-parquet/), [Export to Avro](/reference/workflow-steps/export/export-to-avro/) and [How the Data Reaches the Agent](/reference/workflow-steps/agent/agent-remote-import-table-into-sql-database/#how-the-data-reaches-the-agent).

- **ERP posting follows each schedule's mode, and an uncertain answer is held for you to check.** A scheduled event keeps its own posting mode, **Live**, **Simulate** or **Off**, capped by the project's setting, and **Run Selected Events** uses each event's mode. An ERP answer PlaidCloud can't confirm, such as a timeout or a server error, is held as in doubt rather than recorded as rejected, and a posting can't be reversed twice. The Posting Register lists **reversal outstanding** postings, and an Architect authorizes a project to post from **ERP Write Authorizations** on its home page. See [ERP Posting Mode](/administration/scheduled-events/event-scheduler/#erp-posting-mode), [Reversal](/guides/connections/posting-register/#reversal) and [Authorize the Project to Post](/guides/workflows/run-a-workflow/#authorize-the-project-to-post).

- **Row access keeps up with group changes, and says what's wrong.** A group membership that single sign-on sets when someone signs in reaches dashboard row access within about 15 minutes, and a new member of your default groups gets their rows within about a minute. The Row Access window warns when a restriction filters nothing, handles column names containing `&`, `/` or `%`, and shows **Error** with the reason or **Retrying** with a **Retry** button when a sync fails. See [Keeping Dashboards in Sync](/administration/access/managing-security-groups-and-assignments/#keeping-dashboards-in-sync).

- **User-defined functions run on Python 3.14 with newer libraries.** Most functions run unchanged. A function that imports numba, or a standard-library module Python has removed, such as `cgi` or `telnetlib`, now fails and needs rewriting. Forecast's AutoTheta bounds can differ slightly from earlier runs. See [User Defined Transform](/reference/workflow-steps/general/user-defined-transform/).

- **Full agent access is deprecated and will be removed on January 15, 2027.** An LLM step using **Full** access logs a warning. Move to **Read & write**, where agents change data through PlaidCloud's write tools and an LLM step writes only to bindings with **Write** checked. See [Agent Access](/integrations/ai-architecture/llm-providers/#agent-access).

- **Document accounts are easier to set up and browse.** A OneDrive or SharePoint account's file browser opens the folders under its start path. A Google Cloud Storage account needs a valid service-account key, and an existing one without it stops working until you add one. A rejected setting explains itself, and the unused Backup icon is gone from **Manage Accounts**. See [Add Google Cloud Storage Account](/guides/documents/adding-accounts/add-google-cloud-storage-account/) and [Backing Up Document Accounts](/guides/documents/account-management/managing-document-account-backups/).

- **The Project app handles invoices, trips and late time more completely.** A new invoice starts from a master project, which supplies the customer and PO number. Deleting a draft invoice makes its time and trips billable again, and **AR Recipients** sets who is emailed when an invoice is submitted. The trip drawer shows each line's vendor, category and audit status, and **Add Late Time** starts a sheet for an approved week. See [Track Time and Expenses with the Project App](/guides/time-expense/track-time-and-expenses/).

- **The API call that deleted every table no step referred to is removed.** Delete tables by ID instead.

- **Smaller changes around the app.** Double-click a row in an account, dashboard, member, group or setup list to open it. Empty **Dimensions**, **Data Editors**, **UDFs** and **Steps** tabs explain what to do next, and a member with no apps lands on the Launcher. A step's table selector shows when the table was last updated ([Seeing When a Source Table Last Changed](/guides/workflows/create-workflow/#seeing-when-a-source-table-last-changed)). Convert Encoding recognizes Central European, Turkish and Arabic encodings. A link to a removed screen says so.

## Security

- **AI assistants stay within your permissions.** The assistant's data queries are checked by their structure before they run, and only a single read query runs. A read-only AI connection, including Microsoft 365 Copilot, can't export files, save dashboard PDFs, copy rows into another project, or send changes through a REST connection. An import through an AI assistant runs as you, in the project you name, and a conversation can be continued only by the person who started it. The assistant and table references leave out tables, steps, workflows and dimensions your role hides, and Weave counts a failed step you can't open rather than naming it. See [Access Levels](/integrations/microsoft-365-copilot/#access-levels), [What Your Role Hides](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#what-your-role-hides) and [Manage Past Conversations](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#manage-past-conversations).

- **ERP posting can't bypass its controls.** With segregation of duties on, reversals go through the Posting Register, where approval applies. A copied or imported project no longer inherits permission to post, SAP and EBS postings can't be sent to an agent directly, and an SAP Post step in Test Only Mode needs the same permission as a live posting. An undocumented endpoint that posted to Sage Intacct without these checks is removed. Error messages from ERP and REST connections leave out credentials. See [Segregation of Duties](/guides/connections/posting-register/#segregation-of-duties).

- **Secrets are write-only, and only owners decide who uses a connection.** Document account keys, passwords and tokens are never shown again once saved, and changing where an account connects needs the secret again. Passwords and keys in steps live only in variables; secrets already typed into steps were moved into project variables visible to Architects only. Multi-line credentials are masked as you type. Only a connection's owners or a workspace admin can change who has access to it. See [Saved Secrets Are Write-Only](/guides/documents/adding-accounts/#saved-secrets-are-write-only), [Keys Live in Variables](/reference/workflow-steps/document/encrypt-decrypt-file/#keys-live-in-variables) and [Create and Manage a Connection](/guides/connections/create-connection/#security-model).

- **A document account you can't use stays closed to you.** Checking whether a file exists, reading the columns of a Parquet, Avro, ORC or Delta file, filling an import step's columns with **Populate**, and the AI assistant's file inspection now refuse an account you don't have access to, as browsing it already did.

- **Only a workspace admin can change a workspace admin.** Editing, deactivating or deleting an admin, changing their groups, and making someone an admin all need a workspace admin. See [Changing a Workspace Admin](/administration/access/member-management/#changing-a-workspace-admin).

- **A data share speaks only for members of the project it was granted to.** Reading through a share needs a role in the receiving project, and writing needs a **Write** share and the Manager or Architect role there. See [Sharing Data Between Projects](/guides/projects/managing-projects/#sharing-data-between-projects).

- **Restores apply only to the item named.** Restoring an earlier version restores the item itself, and the permission check is on that item. A dimension backup file whose contents don't belong to the dimension in its own header is refused. See [Version History](/guides/projects/managing-projects/#version-history).

- **Templates, imports and archives refuse unsafe content.** Export templates and report templates render in a sandbox that reads only the data they're given. An import builds its loading query from its own settings, a project archive holding content a PlaidCloud archive never contains is refused, and a project slug that would share another project's reporting schema is refused. See [What a Template Can Use](/reference/workflow-steps/export/export-templated/#what-a-template-can-use) and [Restoring an Archive](/guides/projects/archive-a-project/#restoring-an-archive).

- **Dashboards follow PlaidCloud sign-in and your row access.** A dashboard's thumbnail is rendered as you, accounts come from PlaidCloud sign-in only, and dashboards import only from the ZIP file that Export produces. See [Importing Existing Dashboard](/guides/dashboards/learning-dashboard/#importing-existing-dashboard).

- **Starting a workflow run no longer returns the run's credential.**

- **The environment that runs user-defined functions no longer includes PySpark's Java libraries,** which carried known vulnerabilities. PySpark's DataFrame and SQL API still reaches your Spark Compute Cluster, but a function that imports `SparkContext` or `RDD` now fails. See [Getting Started with PySpark](/integrations/pyspark/getting-started/).

- **Attachments, restores and conversions read only accounts you can use.** Emailing a file, restoring a dimension or a table archive from a file, inspecting or importing a table bundle, filling an Excel export from a template, and converting an Alteryx workflow now refuse a document account you don't have access to. Backing up a dimension to a document account needs write access to it. Workflow steps are unaffected, except that a step emailing a file, restoring tables or filling an Excel template can no longer read from a Personal Temp Drive.
