## Added

- **Use your own Snowflake or Databricks warehouse, connected by you.** A workspace administrator opens **Use your own warehouse** in the user menu, copies a generated setup script into their own account (it creates a capped warehouse and a service identity for PlaidCloud and never touches existing warehouses), then pastes the key pair or Databricks OAuth secret and chooses **Use as my warehouse**. **Check connection** explains any problem and its fix in plain words, with ready-to-run `GRANT` statements. PlaidCloud connects from `130.211.116.97/32`, which you allow in any network policy. New projects then store their tables and run their workflows in your warehouse, and removing the warehouse revokes PlaidCloud's access there. See [Use Your Own Warehouse](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/).

- **Sign in with Snowflake or Databricks.** Instead of pasting a credential, an administrator attaches the warehouse with **Sign in with Snowflake** or **Sign in with Databricks**, after registering PlaidCloud once in their own account. Each person signs in from **Sign in to your warehouse** in the user menu, and dashboards run with that person's own permissions in the warehouse; a viewer who hasn't signed in is asked to. Workflows run as a PlaidCloud service identity created at attach, which holds only a data role you name; administrator roles are refused. See [Sign In With Snowflake or Databricks](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#sign-in-with-snowflake-or-databricks).

- **Connect more than one warehouse.** **Manage warehouses** in the user menu lists every warehouse with its type, default, project count and status. **Add warehouse** connects another, **Make default** sends new projects to it while existing projects stay where they are, and **Remove** disconnects one that no project uses. See [Manage Warehouses](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#manage-warehouses).

- **See what a run costs on your warehouse.** Run history on a Snowflake or Databricks warehouse gains a **Warehouse cost** column in credits or DBUs. Snowflake costs are *attributed* once Snowflake reports them (the setup script grants the `GOVERNANCE_VIEWER` role for this), and are *estimated* until then; Databricks costs are *estimated*. See [Warehouse Cost per Run](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#warehouse-cost-per-run).

- **Your warehouse's policies and descriptions stay in place.** Schema, table and column descriptions are written to the warehouse's own catalog, and row access and masking policies on Snowflake, and row filters and column masks on Databricks, stay attached when PlaidCloud replaces, recreates or clones a table. See [Your Policies and Descriptions Carry Over](/guides/connections/external-lakehouse/#your-policies-and-descriptions-carry-over).

- **Single sign-on with your own identity provider, set up in PlaidCloud, on paid plans.** On the **Workspaces** page, **Single sign-on** adds Microsoft Entra ID, Okta or any OpenID Connect provider, depending on your plan (the Free plan offers social sign-in only) with a copyable redirect URI and a **Test sign-in** button. People who already have an account link it by signing in to that account once, and new people verify their email unless you choose to trust the provider. See [Single Sign-On](/administration/control-plane/workspace-configuration/#single-sign-on).

- **See which figures PlaidCloud calculated: a ✓ on metric results in the AI assistant.** When the AI assistant in a project answers from an approved metric, the figures now appear in their own card below the answer, with a ✓, what was asked for, when they were calculated, and a note when row access limited them. The ✓ never attaches to the assistant's own text. Draft previews, and metrics that have changed since a conversation was saved, show no ✓ and say why. See [Figures From a Metric](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#figures-from-a-metric).

- **Ask PlaidCloud's own AI assistant for a business number and get the agreed figure.** The AI assistant in a project is now set up to answer a named business number, such as net revenue, from an approved metric definition before writing a query of its own, under your row access, and to turn your words for a breakdown, such as *EMEA*, into the metric's values. It can find and calculate metrics but not create or approve them. See [Business Numbers](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#business-numbers).

- **Run and monitor your workflows from your phone with the PlaidCloud app.** The free PlaidCloud app is on the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6769620647) for iPhone and iPad and on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.plaidcloud.mobile) for Android. Enter your workspace name and sign in on your workspace's own sign-in page, single sign-on included. Browse your projects and search their workflows, see each workflow's state and last run, and **Run**, **Pause**, **Resume** or **Stop** it. You can follow each step's state, look back through run history, and rename, describe, enable or lock a project, workflow or step. On iPhone and iPad, a **Home** tab lists failed workflows and your recent projects, and each past run shows its errors and log. The app uses your existing workspace and permissions. See [The PlaidCloud Mobile App](/guides/mobile-app/).

- **Work with PlaidCloud data in Excel with PlaidXL.** The PlaidCloud add-in for Excel signs you in with your PlaidCloud email and your usual sign-in, single sign-on included, and lists your projects in a task pane. Choose a table's columns, filter its rows, and retrieve it into a worksheet with a frozen header row, filters and a named range; **Refresh All Tables** brings every retrieved table up to date. `PLAIDXL` formulas list projects, tables and dimensions, lay out and drill a hierarchy, and calculate a measure rolled up to any combination of dimension nodes, so a report grid recalculates against current data. **Sanitize Workbook** turns a copy into plain values for sending. See [Work in Excel With PlaidXL](/get-started/tutorials/excel-with-plaidxl/) and the [PlaidXL reference](/reference/cli/plaidxl/).

- **Use PlaidCloud in your own language.** A **Language** button in the top header, next to **Appearance**, lists English (US), English (UK), Deutsch, Español, Français, Português (Portugal), Português (Brasil), Polski, 日本語, 한국어 and 简体中文 by their own names. Pick one, confirm that PlaidCloud will reload in it (unsaved changes are lost), and the interface changes. Your choice is saved to your profile, so it follows you to other devices and browsers from their next page load; it applies per workspace. Everyone starts in English (US). Numbers and dates follow the chosen language, such as `1.234,5` in German, `1 234 567,89` in Polish and `2026/10/12` in Japanese and Chinese, while values you save, export or send to integrations are unaffected; English (UK) uses British spelling and formats. Japanese, Korean and Chinese input methods work in number and other restricted fields, including full-width digits. Text sent from the server follows your language too, in every language PlaidCloud offers (browsers set to Traditional Chinese fall back to English): system, sign-up, billing and support emails, the sign-in, set-password and signed-out screens, error messages from the interface, the REST API and JSON-RPC, and the project documentation PDF. Screens before sign-in use your browser's language, and sign-up records it for your organization's billing emails. Workflow and step run messages and logs, the activity stream, AI assistant and MCP text, emails you write yourself, the sign-up screens and the content you create, such as project, table and step names, stay in English. See [Change Your Language](/guides/change-your-language/).

- **Workflow texts are delivered through PlaidCloud and confirmed, to members who opted in.** The **Notify: SMS** step now texts workspace members or a distribution list. Each member opts in themselves from **Text alerts** in the user menu, with a US mobile number confirmed by a code, and workspace admins see each member's status on the **Member Info** tab. A text that can't be delivered is reported on the step, which fails by default or warns, and the member receives the message by email instead. Messages can use `{workflow_name}`, `{project_name}` and `{run_url}`, repeats within a set time are collapsed into one, quiet hours hold texts until they end, and **Send test to me** checks your own number. Carrier-gateway texting, which no longer delivers, is gone: a step still saved with a mobile carrier and typed phone number doesn't fail the workflow. With no members or distribution list chosen it completes with a warning, *No text was sent: this step still uses the retired mobile provider and number. Open the step, choose members who've opted in to text alerts, and save it.*, and sends nothing; once you choose recipients it texts them and ignores the leftover carrier and number. See [Notify Via SMS](/reference/workflow-steps/notifications/notify-via-sms/) and [Text Alerts](/guides/workflows/text-alerts/).

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot gives reliable answers and corrects its own mistakes.** Copilot reads the errors and hints PlaidCloud returns, fixes its request and tries again instead of saying the tools are unavailable. For a business question it finds the analysis path and passes the project and table itself, and a filtered total such as one year's EBITDA for a business unit comes back in a single call. An analysis path can be given by id or by name, `query_data_read` can preview only the columns you name, and a misspelled column name lists the closest matches. A person who isn't in a Copilot access group is told to ask an administrator to add them to **Microsoft 365 Copilot Read Only**. Copilot asks once to connect to PlaidCloud; after you choose **Allow**, it answers multi-step questions without repeated confirmations. Administrators update by downloading the Copilot package again and updating the app in Teams; users start a new chat. See [Microsoft 365 Copilot](/integrations/microsoft-365-copilot/) and [Analysis Paths](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/analysis-paths/).
- **Tell AI assistants what an analysis path means, and what each column holds.** Each analysis path has **Prompt Guidance**, opened from **Tools** > **Analysis Paths**, up to 1,000 characters, where you write, one directive per line, the guidance a new analyst would need: the questions the path answers, the filters that always apply, the fiscal calendar and your business terms. Assistants use it to choose the path and then apply it to every query on that path. A column memo set in a table's **Column Properties** now reaches assistants with the table's column list, so a code, a flag or a look-alike measure is read the way you meant it. Assistants here are any LLM connected through PlaidCloud MCP and PlaidCloud's built-in AI; column memos also reach a workflow's LLM step. Prompt Guidance is visible to everyone in the workspace, so leave out anything confidential. Microsoft 365 Copilot picks up the new instructions after an administrator reinstalls the updated Copilot package. See [Making a Path Useful to AI Assistants](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/analysis-paths/#making-a-path-useful-to-ai-assistants).

- **Agree once how a business number is calculated, and every answer from an AI assistant connected over MCP uses that calculation.** A project Architect defines a **metric** — *Net Revenue*, *Headcount*, *Units Shipped* — as a source table, how its rows are added up, the rows that always count, the lookup tables it joins, and the columns it can be broken down by. A second Architect reads the definition and approves exactly what they read; an approval is refused if the definition changed in between, if a table or column no longer exists, or if a joined lookup table has more than one row for a join key. Anyone who reads the project can then ask an AI assistant connected over MCP for the metric by name or synonym, broken down by the columns it allows, and the figures are calculated by the approved definition under their own row access, not worked out by the assistant. Editing an approved metric starts a new draft while the approved version keeps answering; retiring one takes it out of service for good. Definitions are managed through an AI assistant over MCP or the REST API. An analysis path's Prompt Guidance keeps its standing filters and terminology; for an assistant connected over MCP, the number itself now belongs to a metric definition. See [Metric Definitions](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/).

- **Build a metric from two others, such as net revenue per FTE, and it is calculated the agreed way too.** A project Architect can define a derived metric as the ratio, sum or difference of two metrics, or one metric multiplied by a fixed factor, and a second Architect approves it as usual once its inputs are approved. Each input is totaled on its own, under the asker's row access, before the two are combined, so a ratio of figures from two tables never double-counts, and a breakdown value one input has no rows for, or a division by zero, comes back empty rather than as a zero. A derived metric can be broken down only by the columns every input allows. A metric that an approved derived metric is built from can't be retired, and approving a new version of it changes the derived figures with it. See [Derive a Metric From Others](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#derive-a-metric-from-others).

- **Ask a metric for its variance against budget, the top or bottom N, or each row's share of the total, and get the agreed calculation every time.** A project Architect can give a metric a scenario column that separates actual rows from budget rows; a question can then ask for the variance and gets the actual figure, the budget figure, the difference and the percentage of budget for each breakdown value, each side totaled on its own. Any metric can be asked for the N highest or lowest figures, per group if wanted, with ties kept, and for each row's share of the total over the whole breakdown or over a group. A share is always of the rows the asker can see, and the answer says when row access limited them. A metric can also be defined as a weighted average, such as unit price weighted by units. A question takes at most one comparison and one ranking. See [Compare, Rank and Share](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#compare-rank-and-share).

- **Ask a metric in your own words and get the agreed values, never a guess.** An AI assistant connected over MCP now finds a metric by part of its name or a synonym, in any case. A project Architect can bind a metric's breakdown column to a dimension, and the words in a question are then matched against that dimension's member names and aliases: a member becomes its value, a parent such as *EMEA* becomes every region beneath it, and a word that matches nothing, or more than one member, comes back as candidates for the assistant to ask about rather than a choice made for you. Where the column is governed by row access, only the values your access allows are offered. See [Ask About a Metric](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#ask-about-a-metric).

- **Ask a metric about one fiscal period, compare it with last year or with the quarter or month before, and get a running total through the year.** A project Architect can tell a metric definition where its rows sit in the fiscal calendar: a date column with the month the fiscal year starts in, or fiscal year and period columns on a 12-period, 13-period, weekly or quarterly calendar. A question can then name a fiscal year, a quarter or a period, or the year to date, and ask for the figure year over year, quarter over quarter or period over period. The two periods are each totaled on their own, so a month with no rows last year compares as empty rather than as the nearest month that had some, and the answer says which earlier period it compared with. A running total gives each period's figure and the total so far, for sum and count metrics. See [Compare Over Time](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#compare-over-time).

- **Moving a project to Lakehouse v2 is guided from readiness to undo.** **Check Migration Readiness** reports each area — tables, views, steps, dashboards, data shares and capacity — as clear, needs review, blocked or checked during migration, with a plain-language explanation of every finding; an area PlaidCloud can only check while it migrates is never shown as clear, and a project already on Lakehouse v2 is told it has nothing to migrate. A project that other projects read through a data share is migrated after those projects, or after the share is removed. **Try on v2…** makes a private trial copy of the project on Lakehouse v2 with schedules and sensors turned off and ERP posting in simulate mode. A migration can start now or at a time you choose. The project's own workflow schedules pause while it runs and are restored afterwards; writes made during a migration are lost, so a workflow started then shows a warning. When it finishes, a **What Changed…** report lists each table's outcome, the views that were skipped and the schedules restored, and **Undo Migration…** returns the project to Lakehouse v1 within the undo window after you type the project name. A failed migration shows why it failed and what will be lost, and offers **Resume**. You are notified, by message and by email, when a migration finishes or fails. Views that can't run on Lakehouse v2 are skipped and listed, JSON values keep their types, and a number inside JSON too large for exact storage is stored with double precision and reported. **Migrate Project to Lakehouse v2…** appears for members who can edit projects, and only a project's Architects can migrate it — start, schedule, cancel or undo — and the project must be unlocked. Before a run starts, PlaidCloud checks that your plan has room for the copy: on a plan with a storage limit it refuses a move that wouldn't fit, counting other migrations in progress, and on a plan billed for extra storage it warns you of the expected overage. A migration can't be stopped once it is switching over, and if the switch-over itself fails partway, the run shows **Contact support. Don't retry.** instead of offering **Resume**. A finding you accept is accepted for that item only. See [Migrate a Project to Lakehouse v2](/guides/data/migrate-project-lakehouse/).

A review step before the copy shows its size, the number of tables, a rough estimate, the target lakehouse and any warnings. A copy with data is private to the person who made it, and a copy of a project with row-level security stays private. A **Copy Results** window lists each table's outcome by name with the reason for any failure, and **Retry failed tables** copies only those. A project's **Default Dashboard Audience** sets who sees its new dashboards from their first publish, and the AI assistant manages audiences too. - **The fix assistant answers common errors straight away.** When a step fails because an expression or its column mapping names a column that isn't in the table it reads, a setting it needs was left empty, a workflow it runs failed, or a table it names can't be found, the first reply in **AI Assistant** explains what usually causes that error, which words in the error message to look at, and how to fix it. A SQL Extract step gets the same for SQL that can't be read as written, a function the database doesn't recognize, a table that doesn't exist, a function given a type of value it can't work with, or a value that can't be converted to the type asked for. A Table Extract step gets the same for a source table that doesn't exist, an expression using a function the database doesn't recognize, a function given a value of the wrong type, and a step with no output columns, with fixes that point to the step's own tabs and fields. Reply to have the assistant look at the step itself and propose a change. See [Common Errors](/guides/ai-assistant/fixing-workflow-errors/#common-errors).

- **Fix a Weave finding with the AI fix assistant, straight from the Weave tab.** Click **Fix** on a problem's card in a project's **Weave** tab to open the fix assistant on it, in a window of its own. **Fix** is offered for **Allocation column mapping** and **Step expressions** problems, and for **Last run failed** when one step failed in that run. The assistant starts from the problem as Weave reports it now, proposes a change, and applies it only once you approve. Closing the chat reads Weave's findings again, so a problem the fix resolved leaves the list. See [Fix a Finding with the AI Assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#fix-a-finding-with-the-ai-assistant).

- **The fix assistant re-runs the step it fixed, when you ask it to.** After it applies a fix, the assistant asks whether to re-run the step. Say yes and it runs the step and tells you whether it succeeded, finished with a warning, or failed again, and it diagnoses a new failure. It doesn't offer a re-run for a step that posts to an ERP system or runs another workflow, which you re-run from its workflow, or when your role can't run workflows. - **See everything Weave did in a project, who approved it, and export it for your auditors.** Click **Activity** on a project's **Weave** tab to see, newest first, the problems Weave noticed and later stopped finding, each change the AI assistant proposed, who requested and who approved or rejected it, exactly what each change replaced and wrote, and how it ended, along with changes to a table's freshness threshold. Filter by workflow, step, person and period, and click **Export CSV** or **Export PDF** to save the period as audit evidence, headed by who exported it and a tamper check. Entries can't be edited or deleted, and an entry about something your role can't open shows only that something happened, when, and who did it. Weave now records your approval before it makes a change, and makes no change it can't record. See [Weave Activity History](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-activity/).

- **Weave findings have owners and comments.** Each finding on the Weave tab shows who owns it — by default whoever last ran its workflow — and **Assign** hands it to anyone who can see it. When a run ends, Weave tells each problem's owner. Add comments to a finding to say what's happening with it; comments can't be edited or removed, and appear in the project's Weave activity history with each change of owner. See [Give a Finding an Owner](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#give-a-finding-an-owner).

- **Weave tells you when a table hasn't been written on schedule.** For a table that a scheduled workflow writes, Weave checks that a step has written it since the schedule last came due, and reports the time of the last write when none has. A write by any step counts, including a run you started yourself. A table is reported as unchecked rather than late while a workflow that writes it is running, when a step that writes it was skipped or can be skipped without a record, or when its schedule runs less often than every 180 days. See [What Weave Checks Today](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#what-weave-checks-today).

- **Set how often a table must be written, and Weave checks it.** Give a table a freshness threshold, from 1 hour to 180 days, and Weave reports it when no step has written it for longer than that. The threshold replaces the schedules of the workflows that write the table, so it can be looser or tighter than they are, and it lets Weave check a table that no scheduled workflow writes. Weave can't see when a table that no step writes, such as one you load by hand, was last loaded, so it reports that table as unchecked. Set or clear a threshold under **Freshness** in a table's **View/Edit Table Details** window, through an AI assistant connected through PlaidCloud MCP, or through the platform API. See [What Weave Checks Today](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#what-weave-checks-today).

- **Weave tells you when a table a workflow reads has been written to since it last read it.** For a workflow that reads a table another workflow writes, Weave reports when the other workflow has written to the table since the first one last read it, so its results may be out of date. Where a schedule runs the first workflow, Weave waits until that schedule has come due since the write. A workflow's own writes don't count, including those of a workflow it runs, and neither does a staging table it writes before reading. Where Weave can't follow what a step reads, such as an AI step or a table picked with a variable, it reports the workflow as unchecked rather than passed. See [What Weave Checks Today](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#what-weave-checks-today).

- **Weave tells you when a step's run changes how a column's values spread.** After each run, Weave compares every table the step wrote with the table as it stood before the run, and reports a decimal-number column whose values spread very differently, with a shift score where 0.25 or more is a large change. It compares with the last version that held rows, so the first good run after a failure is judged against the last good data. Whole-number columns, usually codes, periods or flags, are not compared. A table with Row Access, with more than 5,000,000 rows, that takes longer than 10 seconds to compare, or whose earlier version is no longer kept, is reported as unchecked rather than passed. See [What Weave Checks Today](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#what-weave-checks-today).
- **See what a change to a step affects before you save it.** Open a saved step and, above **Save**, Weave says how many steps downstream read what it writes and in how many workflows: "Changes to this step affect 8 downstream steps in 2 workflows." It counts the steps that read the tables this step writes and the steps that read theirs, up to ten steps on. **Show the steps** lists them by workflow, and **How Weave knows** says which tables it followed and what it didn't check: dashboards, exports and anything outside the project, and whether those steps would fail rather than just run on different data. A chain longer than ten steps is reported as a floor, a step your role hides is counted but not named, and a step that writes no table says so. The sentence comes from your project's saved settings, with no AI involved, and it never blocks the save. See [What a Change Affects](/guides/ai-assistant/impact-preview/).

- **See how Weave knows what it tells you.** Every Weave finding, and every answer from the AI assistant, has a **How Weave knows** panel. For a finding it shows the check Weave ran, the values it used, where they came from — a run, the run history, a step's settings, a table — with a link to open each, and what the check leaves out. For an answer it lists what the assistant did, shows each query it ran, and marks each sentence **Matched**, **Unmatched** or **Not checked**, according to whether its figures and names appear in what the assistant read. Matched is not a guarantee: Weave doesn't check that a figure is attached to the right name. The panel is built without AI and names only what you can see. See [How Weave Knows](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#how-weave-knows) and [How Weave Knows in the AI Assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#how-weave-knows).
- **StarRocks lakehouses handle JSON columns, Delete Rows, aggregates in conditions and `to_char` masks.** A JSON column is stored and read back as JSON. **Table: Delete Rows** deletes the rows you select from a StarRocks table. A condition or **Raise Error** check whose expression aggregates, such as a `sum` over a table, evaluates. `to_char` with a digit mask such as `'9'` or `'000'` gives the same text as on Databend. An upsert reports the row count of the load it just ran, not a stale one.

- **Review one workflow before you rely on it.** A Weave review runs Weave's checks on a workflow and its steps, plus three it runs only when asked: an allocation whose output flows back into the table it allocates or its driver table, an allocation driver table or column that doesn't exist, and a table the workflow writes with a column that row-level security doesn't filter on. Its problems come first, and it lists what it doesn't check, such as whether every amount being allocated finds a matching driver row. Choose **Check** on a workflow's canvas or **Steps** grid, ask an AI assistant connected through PlaidCloud MCP to review the workflow, or call the platform API. In the app and through the API, a review that finds problems also carries a short summary written by PlaidCloud's built-in AI, which is shown only the review's problems and is dropped if it quotes a name or a number they don't contain. See [Reviewing One Workflow](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#reviewing-one-workflow).

- **See your own data at a glance as soon as you import it.** After **Bring your own data** imports a CSV, a **Your data at a glance** panel shows the table's row count, each column's type, share of empty values and number of distinct values, and a starter bar chart. When there is a choice of what to chart, PlaidCloud's AI picks it from the column names, types and counts alone, never your values. Where reporting is on and you can publish tables, PlaidCloud also builds a starter dashboard from the chart, one click away under **Open dashboard**. [Start with your own CSV](/get-started/quickstart/#start-with-your-own-csv)

A table name already in use gets a number, such as `sales_2`, and a failed import leaves your project as it was. **New** > **Project from CSV File** on the **Projects** screen creates a project from a CSV, so you no longer need an empty workspace or the welcome panel to get there.   - [Report: Generate Single](/reference/workflow-steps/reports/report-single/) and [Report: Generate Batch](/reference/workflow-steps/reports/reports-batch/) — source, constant and batch column names must each be distinct and readable by the template: letters, digits and underscores, starting with a letter or underscore, and not one of the eight words the template reserves, such as `true` or `self`. New sources are named `new_source`, `new_source_2` and so on.
  - [Table: Union Distinct](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-union-distinct/) and [Table: Union All](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-union-all/) — every source must produce the first source's columns.
  - [Table: Pivot (Rows to Columns)](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-pivot/) — the category and value columns must be among the step's output columns.
  - [Table: Faker](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-faker/) — each fake data column needs a name of its own.
  - [SAP: Attach File to Document](/reference/workflow-steps/sap/call-sap-financial-document-attachment/) — the output columns must include `relative_file_path`, as text.
  - [SAP PCM: Hyper Loader](/reference/workflow-steps/sap-pcm/run-sap-pcm-hyper-loader/) — each source must match its loader table, and two sources can't share a name.
  - [SCD-2 As-Of Join](/reference/workflow-steps/dimensions/scd2-asof-join/#whats-checked-when-you-save) — a column takes one role, once, and a **Literal As-Of Date** must be a real date in YYYY-MM-DD form.
  - [Document: Save Dashboard as PDF](/reference/workflow-steps/document/dashboard-to-pdf/#whats-checked-when-you-save) — at least one **Column Filter**, each with a **Mapped Filter Column** that is one of the dashboard's filters, and only one **File Name**.
  - [Forecast](/reference/workflow-steps/general/forecast/) — the mapping needs source columns.
  - [Text: NLP (AI)](/reference/workflow-steps/text-documents/nlp-ai/) — a source table is required, and an ID column can't share its name with a column the task writes.
  - [AI: LLM Step](/reference/workflow-steps/general/llm-step/#whats-checked-when-you-save) — a result schema must be a JSON object declaring `"type": "object"`, and bindings need an Anthropic connection, whose **Agent Access** allows writes for a binding with **Write** checked.
  - [Table: Append](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-append/) and [Table: Upsert](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-upsert/#whats-checked-when-you-save) — when the target table already exists, the mapping must fit its columns and types, and an upsert must map every one of its columns. Numeric and currency count as the same type.

  An Alteryx workflow is imported through the same checks. Re-importing one into a project whose Append or Upsert target tables already exist with different column types now fails the import, rather than that step's first run.

- **Saving a step with an empty column mapping fills it from its source**, as the old-style form did. In **Table: Append**, **Table: Upsert**, **Table: Pivot (Rows to Columns)**, **Table: Melt (Columns to Rows)**, **SAP: Attach File to Document**, and each source of **Table: Union Distinct** and **Table: Union All**, saving with a source table chosen but no source or no target columns fills the empty side, as **Populate Both Mapping Tables** would. **Report: Generate Batch** fills its batch mapping the same way.

Such a step now copies into its own project, without needing to be opened and saved again. It also reads its source through a data share: copying from a project that shares with this one used to fail with *You do not have access to this project*, while Import Project Table, reading through the same share, worked. A copy needs the source project to allow the target to **Read** and, when it writes into another project, that project to allow this one to **Write**. A run without a share it needs fails, saying which share to add, and saving such a step is refused. A copy within one project needs no share, and a refused copy no longer leaves an empty target table behind. See [Data Sharing Management](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/frame-project-copy/#data-sharing-management).

- **Full agent access is deprecated and will be removed on January 15, 2027.** Until then, an LLM connection's **Agent Access** and a Microsoft 365 Copilot user's access level can still be set to **Full**, and an LLM step that uses Full access logs a warning when it completes. Move to **Read & write**: agents then make changes through PlaidCloud's write tools rather than with SQL that writes, and an LLM step writes only to bindings with **Write** checked, and cannot run SQL that writes or create tables. See [Agent Access](/integrations/ai-architecture/llm-providers/#agent-access).

- **A member with no apps lands on the Launcher**, which says it has nothing to show yet, instead of a blank **Home** tab.

- **Report: Generate Batch no longer shows Report Constants.** Its **Report Constants (Fixed Values)** section was never used: each batch row supplies the template's values. See [Reports Batch](/reference/workflow-steps/reports/reports-batch/).

- **Manage Accounts no longer shows a Backup icon.** Backup sets were never available, and the window it opened could not load. To keep a copy of an account's files in another account, use a scheduled workflow — see [Backing Up Document Accounts](/guides/documents/account-management/managing-document-account-backups/).

- **A link to a screen PlaidCloud no longer has says so.** Opening a bookmark or saved link to a screen that has been removed shows *This screen is not available.* rather than trying to open it.

- **User-defined functions run on newer Python libraries, without numba.** The environment that runs user-defined functions now has pandas 2.3.3, NumPy 2.5, statsforecast 2.1 and newer releases of many of the other libraries it offers. numba is no longer included, so a function that imports it now fails and needs rewriting without it. If a function relies on how an older release behaved, check its results after the update. See [User Defined Transform](/reference/workflow-steps/general/user-defined-transform/).

- **User-defined functions run on Python 3.14.** They ran on Python 3.12 until now, and most run unchanged. Python has removed some old standard-library modules since then, among them `cgi`, `crypt`, `imghdr`, `pipes`, `telnetlib`, `audioop` and `lib2to3`, so a function that imports one now fails with `ModuleNotFoundError` and needs rewriting without it. Python's release notes for [3.13](https://docs.python.org/3/whatsnew/3.13.html#removed-modules-and-apis) and [3.14](https://docs.python.org/3/whatsnew/3.14.html#removed) list everything removed. See [User Defined Transform](/reference/workflow-steps/general/user-defined-transform/).

- **Forecast's AutoTheta bounds can differ slightly from earlier runs.** When the **Model** is **AutoTheta**, the 95% lower and upper bounds a forecast writes can differ slightly from earlier runs of the same step, because the forecasting library now calculates them differently. The forecast values are unchanged, and so are the other models' bounds. See [Forecast](/reference/workflow-steps/general/forecast/).

- **Convert Encoding, Convert to UTF-8 and Convert to UTF-16 recognize more legacy encodings.** When a source file isn't UTF-8, these steps now identify Central European, Turkish and Arabic encodings they used to misread. They work the encoding out from the file's content, so a very short or highly repetitive file can be read wrongly — check the converted output of a file like that. See [Convert Document Encoding](/reference/workflow-steps/document/convert-document-encoding/).

- **Every step that maps columns flags a source column that is no longer listed.** On Table Extract, Table Append, Table Union All and the other steps that map source columns to target columns, a target row whose **Source Column** the source columns no longer list now shows the column name in red behind a ⚠ warning sign. Saving with such a row opens the **Stale Columns Found** dialog the join and lookup steps already use, with **Remove All and Save**, **Save Anyway** or **Cancel**. Before, the step saved without a word and failed when it ran. Rows with a constant, an expression or a **Count (including nulls)** summary are never flagged. Table Update checks its **Update Column** on every row. See [Source Columns That Are No Longer Listed](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-extract/#source-columns-that-are-no-longer-listed).

## Fixed

- **Tables and code in AI answers can be read in the Dark theme.** In the Dark theme, a table's header row and a code block in an answer from Weave AI — in the Weave tab, the fix assistant and the Expression Editor's AI panel — kept a light background behind light text, so they couldn't be read, and the table's border barely showed. They now have a dark background, and the border shows against the page. See [Weave AI Chat](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#weave-ai-chat).

- **Join, lookup and spatial steps no longer delete mapping rows on their own.** Opening a Table Inner, Outer, Anti or Cross Join, Table Lookup, Spatial Find Nearest or Spatial Match step, or changing its source columns, used to delete every Join Map and output column row that named a column missing from the source columns, with only a brief *Stale rows removed* notice. Rows with a constant or expression were deleted too, although the step never reads their source column. Now each such row stays where it is, with the column name in red behind a ⚠ warning sign that explains the problem when you hover over it. Saving with flagged rows opens a **Stale Columns Found** dialog that offers **Remove All and Save**, **Save Anyway** or **Cancel**. See [Table Inner Join](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-inner-join/#stale-columns).

- **REST Request fan-out sends query values once-encoded.** A `{{row.column}}` value in a query parameter was URL-encoded twice, so the server received `A%201` for `A 1` and `a%2Fb` for `a/b`. It is encoded once now. Values made only of letters, digits and `-._~` are unchanged. A POST, PUT or PATCH step with **Add Idempotency-Key header** on and such a value in its query gets a new key once after the upgrade, because the request changed, so a re-run that straddles the upgrade can send one repeat request for each such row.

- **A file import set to skip when unchanged re-imports into an emptied table.** The skip now applies only while the target table still holds the rows the last import loaded, so an emptied table no longer stays empty with every downstream step running on nothing. See [Import CSV](/reference/workflow-steps/import/import-csv/).
- **Changing a workflow from Standard to Advanced through the API or an AI assistant keeps the step order.** It now behaves like **Convert to Advanced…**, and changing an Advanced workflow back to Standard is refused. See [Advanced Workflows](/guides/workflows/advanced-workflows/#choose-the-workflow-type).
- **Run Selected Events uses each event's ERP posting mode.** Starting scheduled events on demand from the Event Scheduler ran them Live, whatever posting mode each event was set to. Each now runs in its own mode, still capped by the project's setting. See [Running Events on Demand](/administration/scheduled-events/event-scheduler/#running-events-on-demand).
- **Adding a workspace member who already has an account now works.** **Add Workspace Member** failed with "Internal server error" for anyone who already had an account, including people whose account was created the first time they signed in with your single sign-on. The add now succeeds: it applies the Builder role or project access you chose, never lowers a project role they already hold, and sends them a welcome email so they know they have access. A deactivated member, a system account, or an account with an unverified email is refused with a clear reason. **Resend Welcome Email** is refused for someone who has already set a password or signed in, and reports a delivery failure instead of success. See [Member Management](/administration/access/member-management/).
- **Report: Generate Batch with an empty batch table finishes with a warning.** A run whose batch table has no rows used to fail with *pod exited without writing a result*. It now completes, warning that the batch table is empty and naming it, and produces no reports. See [Reports Batch](/reference/workflow-steps/reports/reports-batch/).
- **The AI assistant's `workflow_run` tool starts at the step you name.** `from_step_id` begins the run at that step, that step included; it used to be ignored and the run started at the beginning. Naming several `steps` runs them together as one run on an Advanced workflow, and a Standard workflow refuses with a message to name one step or use from and to.
- **Starting a parallel Standard workflow from a step no longer reruns the steps before it.** A Standard workflow whose execution method is **parallel**, started from a step through the AI assistant's `workflow_run` tool with `from_step_id` or through the API, ran every step again, including those before the one you named. It now runs that step and the steps after it, as a **serial** workflow does, and resuming the run stays within those steps.
- **A run that starts inside a parallel group now finishes.** On a Standard workflow, a run that began at a step inside a group set to **Execute in Parallel** ran that one step and then never finished. This happened with **Run Selected Step(s)**, with a serial workflow started from a step through the AI assistant's `workflow_run` tool or the API, and when resuming a serial run that had stopped inside such a group. Each now runs the steps it covers and finishes. A selection that ends inside a parallel group also no longer runs the group's steps listed after it, and the run waits for every selected step in the group to finish before it ends.
- **Row Access handles column names with special characters, and its sync status says what is wrong.** A table with a column named like *Fiscal Year & Period*, with an `&`, `/`, `(` or `%` in the name, no longer raises an error such as "Period is not defined" when you pick the column to restrict, preview a table for a group, or save. A restricted column that also sits on a table that was never published now ends in **Error** naming that table, instead of staying on **Waiting**; publish the table or restrict the column on a published table. A sync being retried after a failure reads **Retrying** with the reason and a **Retry** button. See [Keeping Dashboards in Sync](/administration/access/managing-security-groups-and-assignments/#keeping-dashboards-in-sync).

- **The AI assistant changes nothing until you approve, and shows table and column names as written.** Asking it to build a step into a new table used to create the table before you had approved anything. The proposal now says the table is new and where it will go, and the table and step are created together only after you approve. Creating a project, workflow or dimension, copying a table, changing table properties, and adding or removing labels also ask first. An approval counts only in your very next reply, and if you change the request, the assistant shows you the new version to approve instead of building the old one. Proposals name tables rather than showing internal ids. In every AI chat, including the expression assistant, names with underscores such as `agg_freight_by_country` display exactly, instead of turning into italics with the underscores dropped. See [Building a Workflow Step](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#building-a-workflow-step).

Two different duplicate steps in one workflow are now separate findings, so marking one read no longer hides the other. Some findings you had already read can show as unread once after this update. See [What Weave Checks](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/).

A repeated source column no longer stops either. A value in place of a condition is reported as a problem with that clause, because the step fails when it runs, and saving the step says so; use `False`, `True` or a condition instead. The menu on a suggested expression — **Insert at Cursor**, **Append**, **Replace**, and **Copy to Clipboard** — now works on every reply. And the panel's prompt box shows its "Ask Weave AI" hint in a lighter color, as every placeholder hint across PlaidCloud now does, instead of looking like text already typed.

A header with leading or trailing spaces or a line break no longer makes the import fail intermittently with "Table … is not found" or "Unknown column"; the spaces and line breaks are trimmed from the column name. A value that cannot be converted, such as non-numeric text cast to a number, becomes null. Float columns PlaidCloud creates are double precision, and an existing table rebuilt with **Overwrite** becomes double precision. See [Casting to Float](/reference/workflow-steps/common/advanced-data-mapper-usage/#casting-to-float).
- **An import whose target columns are all fixed values loads every row.** An import step in which every target column's expression is a constant, such as `cast('1225541207.71', Float)`, with none of them reading a source column, failed. It now writes one row for each source row, or for each row its source filter keeps. See [Import CSV](/reference/workflow-steps/import/import-csv/).

Any answer PlaidCloud can't confirm — a timeout, a server error, a status it doesn't recognize — is now held as in doubt for you to check. It also no longer rejects a what-if answer's own wording when a result's name contains a dollar amount. See [The Written Summary Is Checked, Not Just Written](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/honest-answers/#the-written-summary-is-checked-not-just-written).

- **A crash in a database driver fails only the step that hit it.** A driver failure, such as SQL Server refusing a connection's login because the password was changed at the source, used to stop the PlaidCloud service running the step, and any other steps running there at the same time stopped too, with each retry stopping it again. The step now fails with the database's own reason, such as *Login failed for user*, and nothing else is affected. If the password has changed, update it on the connection.

- **Exports to a remote database commit their rows.** An export to a remote database other than SQL Server could be rolled back, so rows the step reported as written were missing from the target. They are now committed when the export finishes.

- **A Workflow Loop runs the looped workflow on every iteration again.** Only the first iteration ran the looped workflow's steps; every later one finished at once without running them, while the loop still reported each iteration as done. The same applied to an iterating container in an Advanced workflow, to a step that a Goto sent the workflow back to, and to a workflow that Run Workflow steps ran more than once in the same run. Each of them now runs its steps every time. See [Workflow Loop](/reference/workflow-steps/workflow-control/workflow-loop/).

- **An allocation no longer fails when a dimension-type property's Hierarchy is "Not Specified".** A node property that picks members from a dimension can leave **Hierarchy** as **Not Specified**, so the picker shows every hierarchy. A rule using such a property failed with an error if that dimension still contained members belonging to no hierarchy, which older dimensions can carry. Those rules now run. A member no hierarchy contains also no longer stands in for itself when a hierarchy *is* named, so it contributes nothing to the rule rather than only itself. See [Dimension-Type Properties and the Member Picker](/guides/dimensions/dimensions/#dimension-type-properties-and-the-member-picker).

- **Multi-Table Join conditions accept column names with spaces and punctuation.** A join on a column such as *Build Plant* or *Cust (Id)* used to be refused when the step ran or was saved, with an "Invalid Multi-Table Join configuration" error, even though those columns could already be chosen as outputs. The join now works, and a `BETWEEN` bound written as `alias.column`, such as `cust.Max Id`, is read as a column of that input instead of being compared as text. Names containing a double quote, backtick, backslash or control character are still refused. See [Multi-Table Join](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-multi-table-join/).

- **Reading a large dimension no longer holds up everything else in your workspace.** Tabulating or exporting a large dimension — a hierarchy table, a value's consolidation, an ancestry or level export, a saved hierarchy, a sort of a parent's members, or a bulk alias load — used to stop the server answering anything else for as long as the read took, so other people's requests queued behind it and could time out waiting. Those reads now leave the server free to answer while they run, and consolidating a value is substantially faster in its own right. See [Dimensions](/guides/dimensions/dimensions/).

- **A copied project's schedules arrive paused, and enabling one runs it on the copy.** Copying a whole project into a new project brought its scheduled events across marked active, but they never ran on the copy. They now arrive paused, a message when the copy finishes lists the ones that were active in the original, and enabling one in the **Event Scheduler** runs it on the copy. See [Copying Into a New Project](/guides/projects/managing-projects/#copying-into-a-new-project).

- **Table Lookup ignores an aggregation set on its lookup columns.** A Table Lookup step whose lookup-table columns had an aggregation setting of their own could fail, or return wrong results with no error, such as a row more than once. It now ignores those settings, so each input row comes back once, as in a step without them, and **Export to Portable Format** reproduces the step the same way. See [Table Lookup](/reference/workflow-steps/tables/table-lookup/).

- **A driver what-if from your AI tools no longer answers from a driver your model didn't use.** Asking what a driver change would do (`allocation_driver_scenario`) gave a figure even when the driver table had been edited since the allocation last ran, or when the step filters, limits, de-duplicates, summarizes, computes or converts its driver in a way the estimate can't follow — and that figure could be far from what re-running the model would show. It now checks each group against the split your model actually made, leaves out any group where the two disagree and says how many, and declines when none agree; if the driver changed since the last run, re-run the workflow that builds the allocation, then ask again. Those driver options, and a change that takes a driver value across zero, are declined with the reason. See [Tracing Allocations](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/tracing-allocations/).

- **Editing a schedule through an AI assistant no longer resets what it didn't change.** Updating a scheduled event through the `workflow_schedule` MCP tool with only some of its fields, such as a new name, cleared or reset the rest. A paused event could start running while the **Event Scheduler** still showed it paused, and its memo, timezone and other settings were lost. Fields left out now keep their values, and a new event needs a name and a schedule before anything is saved. See [Editing an Event](/administration/scheduled-events/event-scheduler/#editing-an-event).

- **An Alteryx workflow that records a tool's position with a fraction now converts.** A workflow file that stored a tool's position on the canvas with a fraction, such as `59.99998`, failed to convert, with a message that it wasn't a readable Alteryx workflow. See [Migrate Alteryx Workflows](/guides/workflows/migrate-alteryx-workflows/).

- **AI chat draws diagrams that used to appear as text.** When the assistant wrote a diagram with a stray space or extra words after its opening line, the chat showed the diagram's raw text instead of a picture. PlaidCloud now removes them before the reply is shown, so a diagram of a type the chat can draw appears as a picture. See [Diagrams](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#diagrams).

- **Restoring a table snapshot through the API or an AI assistant keeps a safety snapshot and an audit record.** An in-place restore through the REST API or an AI assistant connected over MCP replaced the table's data with no pre-revert safety snapshot and nothing in the audit log, unlike **Revert / Restore** in the Snapshots panel. It now takes the same safety snapshot, where the storage engine supports one, and writes the same audit record, and passing the newest snapshot you listed as `expected_current_snapshot_id` refuses the restore if the table has been written since. Restoring every table in a project through the API reported success even when some tables weren't restored; it now restores the rest and fails naming each table it couldn't restore and why. On StarRocks, that project restore also took a table back to its state before an earlier revert instead of to its latest hold. A restore to a new table that fails, from the dialog or the API, no longer leaves the new table listed in the project. See [Reverting or Restoring a Snapshot](/guides/data/table-snapshots/#reverting-or-restoring-a-snapshot).

- **The Advanced canvas opens every workflow, and says so when it can't.** A container saved without a position or size, for example one created through the API or MCP, opens drawn around its member steps. A layout item that can't be read no longer stops the workflow opening: a **Layout Partly Unreadable** notification lists what was reset or hidden, and a hidden annotation is removed the next time you save. A step that could end up without a place on the canvas, for example when adding it from the palette was interrupted part-way, is shown below the others; if adding a step loses its place, you're told the step was added and is shown below the others. A workflow that can't be loaded now shows a **Could Not Load Workflow** message instead of a blank canvas, and opening a Standard workflow shows **Visual Canvas Unavailable** with a pointer to **Convert to Advanced...**. See [When the Canvas Can't Open a Workflow](/guides/workflows/advanced-workflows/#when-the-canvas-cant-open-a-workflow) and [Add, Connect, and Edit Steps](/guides/workflows/advanced-workflows/#add-connect-and-edit-steps).

- **Connections of older database types can be deleted.** Deleting a Crate, Apache Drill, Firebird, Impala, MonetDB, SQL Anywhere, SQLite or Sybase/SAP Adaptive Server Enterprise connection from the Connections list failed; it now deletes the connection. These types have no editor, so opening one to edit it says so instead of showing an error.

- **Lists show your change as soon as you save it.** After you added, edited or deleted a scheduled event, edited a user-defined function, or changed a connection's **Members**, **Groups**, **Agents** or **Owners** from the Connections list's **Actions** menu, the list kept showing the old values until you refreshed it. It now updates when you save. Locking or unlocking a workflow, or changing its **Diagnostic Mode**, in the workflow's window also updates the project's workflow list.

- **PlaidLink agents no longer fail to start with `Could not lookup agent by client id: None`.** Some agents couldn't fetch their configuration from PlaidCloud, so they stopped at startup. They now start and run normally, with nothing to change on the agent.

## Security

- **The AI assistant's data queries are checked by their structure before they run.** The assistant's query tool is for reading data. It used to be kept that way by looking for words that change data, and a query could hide those words, for example inside a value passed into the query. Now each query is read in the warehouse's own SQL grammar, with its values filled in, and runs only if it is a single `SELECT`, `WITH … SELECT`, or `SELECT`s joined by `UNION`, `INTERSECT` or `EXCEPT`, with nothing inside it that writes data or locks rows. A query the check can't read is refused, and so are `EXPLAIN`, `SHOW` and `DESCRIBE`.

- **A metric's author can no longer approve their own definition after someone saves it unchanged.** Saving a metric definition without changing anything made the person who saved it the author of the new version, so the Architect who actually wrote the definition could then approve it alone. A save that changes nothing is now refused. See [Who Can Do What](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/#who-can-do-what).

- **The AI assistant and table references leave out what your role hides.** When a table, step, workflow or dimension is hidden from your role, the AI assistant no longer lists it. The steps listed against a table or dimension show one your role hides without its name or workflow, a step you can see no longer shows a workflow your role hides, and a dimension no longer lists a table your role hides. A hidden step still counts when you delete a table, so you're still asked before its references are removed. See [What Your Role Hides](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#what-your-role-hides).

- **Starting a workflow run no longer returns the run's credential.** The API and AI assistants no longer include it in the reply to a run request.
- **Weave no longer names a failed step your role hides.** A workflow's **Last run failed** finding counts the failed steps you can't open rather than naming them, and then doesn't offer **Fix** for that run, since the step to fix could be one you can't open.

- **Only a workspace admin can change a workspace admin.** Editing an admin, changing their email or password, deactivating, re-enabling or deleting them, ending their sessions, changing their groups, and making someone an admin all need a workspace admin. Any other member is refused, and so is a project or agent service account, whatever member permissions it holds. A **Deactivate Workspace Members** step whose list includes admins deactivates everyone else and names the admins it left active. See [Changing a Workspace Admin](/administration/access/member-management/#changing-a-workspace-admin).

- **Export templates read only the data they're given.** An Export Templated step's template, and a templated export through the API or MCP, renders in a sandbox. Each source is a read-only list of rows, where `first()`, `all()`, `mappings()`, `scalar()` and the other read methods still work; with **Use Pandas Dataframes for Table Data** on, it is a read-only table with `columns`, `itertuples()`, `iterrows()`, `head()`, `tail()` and column totals such as `invoices['amount'].sum()`. Other pandas methods, such as `loc` and `groupby`, are no longer available, and any method whose name starts with `to_` is refused. The older REST step's request body and a report's RML template render under the same kind of restriction, so a template that reaches past its data fails the step, and report formulas no longer run as code. See [What a Template Can Use](/reference/workflow-steps/export/export-templated/#what-a-template-can-use).

- **Dashboard thumbnails show only what you can see.** A dashboard's thumbnail is rendered as you, so it follows your row access, and is kept for up to 7 days.

- **Dashboards take accounts from PlaidCloud sign-in only.** Self-registration pages in Superset are closed.

- **Dashboards and datasets import only from the ZIP file that Export produces.** An older JSON or YAML export is refused, so export the dashboard again and import the new file. See [Importing Existing Dashboard](/guides/dashboards/learning-dashboard/#importing-existing-dashboard).

- **Multi-line credentials are masked as you type.** A NetSuite private certificate, an Anaplan certificate and private key, a Postman API key and a Slack or Teams webhook URL show as dots while you paste them, with browser spell checking turned off. Leave one blank when you edit a connection to keep the saved value; a replacement made only of spaces is refused. See [Create and Manage a Connection](/guides/connections/create-connection/).

- **With segregation of duties on, ERP reversals go through the Posting Register.** A reversal requested through the project-level ERP reversal API — the path a project, a user-defined function or a service account uses — is refused, with a message to reverse from the Posting Register, where approval applies. Posting is unaffected, and Business Central, Oracle Fusion and Acumatica keep saying that they don't support reversal. See [Segregation of Duties](/guides/connections/posting-register/#segregation-of-duties).

- **Imports and project archives refuse unsafe content.** An import no longer accepts a loading query from whoever calls it: PlaidCloud builds the query from the import's own settings. A project archive holding files or settings that a PlaidCloud archive never contains is refused before anything is restored. See [Restoring an Archive](/guides/projects/archive-a-project/#restoring-an-archive).

- **An import through MCP runs as you, in the project you name.** The `table_data_import` tool's `options` could replace the person and project an import ran as, so the import was checked against their permissions instead of yours. `options` now only fills in import settings the call leaves out.

- **A read-only AI connection can no longer export files or send changes through a REST connection.** Through Microsoft 365 Copilot or another AI agent connected over MCP, an agent with read-only access could export a table to a file in a document account, save a dashboard as a PDF document, copy a table's rows into another project, or send a request that changes data to an external system through a REST connection. Through MCP, exporting a table to a file or saving a dashboard PDF now needs permission to write documents, copying into another project needs permission to write tables, and a REST connection request other than GET or HEAD needs permission to write connections, so a read-only agent is refused. This applies to people using MCP directly too, for example through the Claude connector: without those permissions, these actions are refused. An AI agent below Full access can also explain-analyze only a single read query through MCP. Exports, dashboard PDFs and REST connection requests in the PlaidCloud app are unchanged. An agent at Read/Write or Full access can still do all of them if the person it acts for has those permissions. See [Access Levels](/integrations/microsoft-365-copilot/#access-levels).

- **A copied or imported project no longer inherits permission to post to an ERP.** Copying a project, or creating one from a project archive, used to carry over the original project's authorization to write to an ERP connection, so the new project could post without an Architect ever authorizing it. An Architect now authorizes each project separately. The project's ERP posting mode is still copied.

- **A data share speaks only for members of the project it was granted to.** Anyone who named the receiving project could act on the sharing project as an Architect — reading its project, table and dimension details, and creating tables and dimensions in it — without a role in either project, and a **Read** share allowed those writes too. No row data was exposed. Reading through a share now needs a role in the receiving project, and writing needs a **Write** share and the Manager or Architect role there. Import Project Table and Export Project Table steps work as before. See [Sharing Data Between Projects](/guides/projects/managing-projects/#sharing-data-between-projects).

- **Restoring an earlier version always restores the item itself.** Restoring a project or dimension version checked permission against the target named in the request but restored the item it was called on, so a Manager on a project that restricts rows could restore that project's settings by naming another project as the target. Restoring a project, dimension, user-defined function, step, workflow or data editor now applies in place, a different target is refused, and the permission check is on the item restored. Through the AI assistant or MCP, `project_versions` restores in place with the target left out, where it used to fail. See [Version History](/guides/projects/managing-projects/#version-history).

- **An undocumented API endpoint that could post to Sage Intacct without the ERP write checks has been removed.** It posted a journal entry without checking that the project was authorized for the connection, and kept no record of the post. Post journal entries with the **Intacct: Post Journal Entry** step, which applies those checks.

- **SAP and EBS postings can no longer be sent to an agent directly.** The agent queue, reached through the API or the MCP `agent_payload` tool, would pass any saved payload to an on-premises agent, so an SAP or EBS posting could skip the ERP write checks, or be sent a second time. The queue now refuses those postings. An SAP function call sent through the queue is now checked against the same list of blocked administrative functions as one from a workflow step.

- **An SAP Post step in Test Only Mode needs the same permission as a live posting.** Test Only Mode still sends the documents to SAP, and whether SAP treats them as a test is up to your SAP posting function. A test run now needs write permission on the SAP connection, or the project's authorization to post to it, just as a live posting does. A test run still doesn't stop a later live run of the same documents.

- **A dimension backup file can only write to the dimension it names.** Restoring a dimension from a backup file applied every entry the file carried, so a file edited by hand could write into a dimension in another project — including one the person restoring it could not otherwise open. A file whose contents do not belong to the dimension named in its own header is now refused, nothing in it is applied, and the dimension being restored is left as it was. Restoring a backup into a *different* dimension, which is supported, is unaffected: it is the file's own contents that have to agree with the file's own header.

- **An AI assistant conversation can be continued only by the person who started it.** Someone who sent the assistant another person's conversation ID, including the ID of a deleted conversation, was given that conversation's history and could add to it. The assistant now refuses, and deleting a conversation removes all of it. See [Manage Past Conversations](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#manage-past-conversations).

- **The environment that runs user-defined functions no longer includes PySpark's Java libraries.** They weren't usable there, and they carried known security vulnerabilities. PySpark's DataFrame and SQL API still reaches your workspace's Spark Compute Cluster, but a UDF that imports `SparkContext` or `RDD`, or passes a Spark DataFrame to statsforecast or Fugue, now fails. See [Getting Started with PySpark](/integrations/pyspark/getting-started/).
