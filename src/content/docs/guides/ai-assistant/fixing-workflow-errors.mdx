## Description

When a workflow step fails or logs a warning, the AI fix assistant reads that
error, works out what caused it, and — when the cause is a misconfiguration of
the step itself — proposes a corrected configuration and applies it once you
approve. It goes beyond explaining the error: it can make the change for you.

It is part of PlaidCloud's [Weave AI](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#weave-ai-chat)
and opens from the project's **Log** viewer, already pointed at the step that
failed. It also opens from a problem in the project's **Weave** tab.

## Opening the Fix Assistant

From the project's **Log** tab, beside the raw log entry:

1. Open the project and click the **Log** tab.
2. In the log list, select the entry for the step that failed. Its full message
   — including any traceback — opens in the panel on the right under the **Log
   Message** tab.
3. Switch that panel to the **AI Assistant** tab.

The **AI Assistant** chat opens already pointed at the workflow and step the
selected log entry came from, so it starts diagnosing straight away — you don't
have to tell it which step failed. You can read the raw error under **Log
Message** and chat with the assistant under **AI Assistant** side by side. For a
few common errors its first reply is an explanation instead — see
[Common Errors](#common-errors).

### From the Weave Tab

In the project's **Weave** tab, click **Fix** on the card of a problem Weave found. The
assistant opens in a window of its own, already pointed at the step the problem is
about, and starts from the problem as Weave reports it now. See
[Fix a Finding with the AI Assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-tab/#fix-a-finding-with-the-ai-assistant)
for which findings offer **Fix**.

## How It Works

1. **Diagnoses** — it reads the failing step's configuration and the tables and
   columns it touches, and checks its claims against your data rather than
   guessing from the error text alone.
2. **Proposes** — if it finds a genuine misconfiguration, it shows the change
   on a card: **Why it failed** in one sentence, then a box for each setting that
   changes, named where it can be as it is on the step's form, with the current value in red and
   the new value in green. Only what changes is listed — for a column list, just
   the columns that change. Nothing is saved at this point.
3. **You approve** — select **Approve N changes** on the card, or **Not now** to leave
   the step as it is. Replying in your own words works too. The buttons become
   available once the assistant has finished its reply, and your approval always
   comes in a message after the proposal.
4. **Applies** — once you approve, it writes the change to the step, through
   the same path the step editor uses. What gets saved is the change on the card,
   and one approval saves it once. Just before saving, it keeps a copy of the
   step's settings as they are, so you can undo the fix — see
   [Undo a Fix](#undo-a-fix).
5. **Offers a re-run** — a second card asks whether to re-run the step now to
   confirm the fix. Approve it and the assistant runs the step and tells you
   whether it succeeded, finished with a warning, or failed again; a new failure
   is an error it then diagnoses. If
   the step hasn't finished within two minutes, it says so, and the result shows in
   the workflow and the **Log** tab.

The assistant doesn't offer a re-run for a step that posts to an ERP system or
runs another workflow — re-run it from its workflow, where you choose the ERP
posting mode — or when the chat isn't tied to a workflow, or when your role can't
run workflows. If the workflow is already running, nothing runs and the assistant
offers to try again.

<Aside>The assistant never changes your step without approval, and this is
enforced by PlaidCloud rather than left to the assistant's judgement. The card
is built by PlaidCloud from your saved step and the proposed one, not from the
assistant's description of the change, and the change is applied only after you
explicitly confirm it in a later message — an ambiguous reply isn't taken as
approval. If it asks you to approve the same change a second time, nothing has
been saved yet.</Aside>

Each proposal, your approval or rejection, and the change itself are recorded in
the project's [Weave activity history](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-activity/),
with exactly what the change replaced and wrote. Your approval is recorded before
the change is made. If it can't be recorded, nothing is saved and the card says
so, and you can approve it again later.

## Undo a Fix

A fix's card says when the fix can be undone: *Weave keeps a copy of the step's
current settings, so you can undo this for a limited time, unless the step is
changed again first.* A card without that line can't be undone. If Weave can't
keep the copy, it saves nothing and says so, and you can ask it again.

Once the fix is saved, the card shows **Undo**. The fix's entry in the project's
[Weave activity history](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-activity/#undo-a-step-fix) has
the same button, so you can undo a fix after its chat is closed. Anyone whose role
can change steps in the project can undo a fix, not only the person who approved
it.

**Undo** opens a window that shows what undoing puts back: each setting the fix
changed, as it is **Now** and as it will be **After Undo**. Then choose:

- **Undo** — put the step's settings back as they were just before the fix.
- **Undo & Run** — put them back, then run the step. It's offered when your role
  can run workflows, the step is still in the workflow the fix was made in (or,
  failing that, in only one workflow) and you can open that workflow, and the step
  doesn't post to an ERP system or run another workflow. The run itself can't be
  undone.
- **Cancel** — leave the step as it is.

Undoing changes the step's settings only. Data that runs of the step have already
written stays as it is: re-run the step to rebuild it.

Weave won't undo a fix, and changes nothing, when:

- **The step has changed since the fix.** Undoing would put back settings that
  are out of date, so the window shows the step as it is **Now** beside its
  settings **Before Weave's Change**, and you can change it yourself.
- **The step already has the settings it had before the fix.**
- **The copy is no longer kept.** Copies are kept for a limited time.
- **Your role can't change the step**, or the project is locked.
- **Weave couldn't record the undo.** Try again later.

An undo is recorded in the activity history as a change of its own, such as
*Done as approved: undo Weave's change to a step's settings.*

## Common Errors

When a step fails with one of these common errors, the first reply is an
explanation PlaidCloud already has for that kind of error, so it appears
straight away.

On any type of step:

- an expression that uses a column that isn't in the table the step reads
- a column mapping whose source column isn't in the table the step reads
- a setting the step needs that was left empty, such as the connection it uses
  or the table it reads
- a workflow the step runs that failed — the cause is most likely in one of
  that workflow's steps, so check that workflow's log
- a table the step names that can't be found
- a Snowflake or Databricks warehouse that blocks PlaidCloud's address, rejects
  its sign-in, has used up its quota, can't be found or used, or is missing a
  privilege PlaidCloud's role needs

On a **SQL Extract** step:

- SQL that can't be read as written, such as a misspelled keyword or a bracket
  that isn't closed
- a function the database doesn't recognize
- a table that doesn't exist
- a function given a type of value it can't work with, such as adding up a text
  column (on Databend)
- a value that can't be converted to the type the SQL asked for, such as text
  where a number or a date was expected (on Databend)

On a **Table Extract** step, with fixes that point to the step's own tabs and
fields rather than to SQL:

- a source table that doesn't exist yet, or no longer does (on Databend)
- an expression that uses a function the database doesn't recognize (on
  Databend)
- a function given a type of value it can't work with, such as adding up a text
  column in an expression or with **Summarize** (on Databend)
- a step with no output columns, such as one whose columns were all removed

The explanation says what usually causes the error, which words in the error
message to look at, and how to fix it. It is general: it doesn't check your step
or change anything. To have the assistant look at the step itself, reply. From
then on it works as described in [How It Works](#how-it-works), with the
explanation as part of the conversation.

Any other error goes straight to the assistant's own diagnosis. That includes
the database errors listed above when they come from any other type of step.

## What It Can Fix

The assistant repairs the configuration of the one step the error came from —
for example a mistyped column or function name, an invalid expression, a wrong
data type or cast, or malformed SQL. It changes the least that fixes the error:
one failure, one targeted change, with the rest of the configuration left as it
was.

An expression that fails — in a column, a filter, or a having clause — is handed
to the same expression specialist that sits beside the Expression Editor, with
the error and the source table's columns. The corrected expression it returns is
checked before it is proposed, and the proposal is still a change to that one
field of the step, approved the same way as any other.

## What It Won't Do

Its only lever is that step's configuration, and it is upfront when the problem
lies elsewhere rather than bending an edit to look like a solution.

- It edits **one step's configuration** and nothing else — it won't create,
  delete, or reorder steps, create tables, edit a different step, or change your
  data.
- Many failures aren't configuration problems: missing or malformed source
  data, an upstream step that failed first, a transient or infrastructure error,
  a permissions issue, broken Python in a UDF or a user-code step, a missing file
  in a document account, or a resource or timeout limit. For these it explains what
  it found and points you to where the real fix lives.
- Warnings are often benign and need no change — it will say so plainly rather
  than invent an edit.
- When it isn't certain a change will work, it says so and names what it's unsure
  about instead of presenting a guess as a certainty.

<Aside type="note">The fix runs with your own permissions on the current
project, so it can only change a step you could already edit yourself. You can
[undo a fix](#undo-a-fix) it saved, or change the step again in the step
editor.</Aside>

## Tips

- The diagnosis is grounded in your live step configuration and table metadata,
  so it reflects the project as it is now — not a cached or assumed shape.
- After a fix is applied, say yes when the assistant offers to re-run the step,
  or re-run it yourself, to confirm the error is resolved.
- If the assistant says the cause is outside the step's configuration (bad data,
  an upstream failure, a permissions problem), that is the answer — fix it where
  it actually lives rather than asking the assistant to edit the step anyway.
