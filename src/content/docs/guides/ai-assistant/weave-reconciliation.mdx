## Description

Before you close, you need to know that one set of figures agrees with another:
the general ledger with the trial balance, a source system with its load, this
month's closing balances with next month's opening ones. A **reconciliation**
names the two sides and how their rows match, and Weave answers in one of three
ways:

> GL Actuals ties out to Trial Balance: all 1,204 accounts agree exactly. Both
> total 12,345,678.90.

> GL Actuals doesn't tie to Trial Balance: 3 of 1,204 accounts differ, by
> 4,210.00 in all ignoring sign. Netted, GL Actuals is 3,900.00 higher. The
> largest is 4000, 3,900.00 apart (GL Actuals 1,000,000.00, Trial Balance
> 996,100.00).

> Weave couldn't check whether GL Actuals ties out to Trial Balance: neither side
> has any rows matching this reconciliation's filters. If that data hasn't been
> loaded yet, load it and check again.

Weave never says a reconciliation ties out when it couldn't read both sides in
full. The comparison is a query on your data; no AI is involved.

## Make a Reconciliation

Only a project Architect can make, edit or delete a reconciliation, and the
project must be unlocked.

1. Open the project's **Weave** tab and click **Reconciliations** in its toolbar.
2. Click **New**.
3. Give it a **Name**, and under **Rows are called** say what one compared row
   is, in the plural — "accounts", or "account and cost center pairs". Weave
   uses it in every sentence; it says "items" when you leave it blank.
4. Under **First side** and **Second side**, give each side a **Label** — the
   name Weave uses for it, such as "GL Actuals" — and pick its **Table**. Under
   **Measure**, choose how its rows add up — **Sum of**, **Count of**, **Distinct
   count of**, **Smallest**, **Largest** or **Average of** — and the column.
5. Add **Filters** to either side to read only some of its rows. Separate several
   values with commas. To compare two periods of one table, pick the same table
   on both sides and filter each side to its period; don't add the period column
   under **Match on**, or no rows will match.
6. Under **Match on**, add one row per key column. Each row pairs a column of the
   first side with a column of the second, so `account` can match `acct`. Give
   the row a heading, such as "Account"; it heads that column in the list of
   differences. With no keys, each side is compared as a single figure — its total,
for a sum or a count.
7. Optionally set a **Tolerance per row**: how far apart a row's two values can
   be and still agree, in the measure's own units, such as `0.01`. It is not a
   percentage. Leave it at 0 to have every row agree exactly.
8. Optionally pick a **Workflow** to put the reconciliation on that workflow's
   close readiness checklist. See [On the Readiness Checklist](#on-the-readiness-checklist).
9. Click **Save Draft**.

Weave refuses a reconciliation it couldn't compare fairly, and says why:

- The two sides need the same number of keys, up to 8, and different labels, and
  they can't be identical apart from their labels.
- Paired key columns must hold the same kind of value — numbers, text, dates,
  dates and times, or true/false. Columns of floating-point numbers (types such as `float` or
  `double`) can't be keys, and a key can't be a column named `value` or `rows`.
- A measure must be a number column, unless it counts.
- An average, or a sum, smallest or largest of a column of floating-point
  numbers, needs a tolerance above 0, because such values rarely agree exactly.
- Every table and column it names must be in the project, and the workflow it is
  linked to must be in the project too.
- Saving with nothing changed is refused.

The two sides may use different measures, such as a sum on one side and a count
on the other. Missing rows count as 0 only when both sides use **Sum of** or
**Count of**; see [Read the Result](#read-the-result).

## Approval

A reconciliation can mark a workflow ready to close, so it takes two people.
What you save is a draft. Another Architect selects it and clicks **Approve**,
and from then on that version is the one readers and the readiness checklist
run. You can't approve a version you were the last to edit, so a project needs
a second Architect to put a reconciliation to use. Approval checks the tables
again, and it is refused if the draft has changed since the approver read it.

Editing an approved reconciliation saves a new draft, and the approved version
stays in use until the new one is approved. When a newer draft is waiting, the
result says so. Architects can click **Check the Draft** to run the working
version before it is approved.

<Aside type="caution">
Deleting a reconciliation takes one Architect. It is the one way a single person
can take a reconciliation that doesn't tie off the readiness checklist: its line
goes with it, and the workflow can then read **Ready**.
</Aside>

## Read the Result

Select a reconciliation to run it. Click **Check Again** to run it after the data
changes.

When it doesn't tie, the rows that differ are listed beneath the sentence, the
largest first, with up to 50 shown. Each shows its keys, both sides' values and
the first side less the second. A row found on only one side shows **no rows**
for the other side, never 0, and a row whose value is empty shows **no value**.
When more rows differ than are shown, a caption says how many are left out and
what they differ by together; add filters to look at fewer rows at a time.

- **Tolerance.** A row agrees when its two values are no further apart than the
  tolerance. Those small differences still add up, so a reconciliation can tie
  out row by row while its totals differ, and the sentence then says so:

  > GL Actuals ties out to Trial Balance: every one of the 1,204 accounts is within
  > its 0.01 tolerance. Across all accounts those small differences net to 0.37:
  > GL Actuals totals 12,345,679.27 and Trial Balance 12,345,678.90, so GL
  > Actuals is 0.37 higher.

- **Missing rows.** When both sides use **Sum of** or **Count of**, a row that only
  one side has counts as 0 on the other side, so a row at 0 on one side and
  absent from the other agrees. A side with no rows at all, while the other has
  some, is always a difference.
- **Other measures.** For a distinct count, the smallest, the largest or an
  average, a row that one side lacks is a difference, and no total is given,
  since those values don't add up.

Weave says it **couldn't check** when it can't compare the two sides in full,
and gives the reason, including when:

- your role can't read table data, or can't see one of the tables a side reads;
- row access lets you see only some of a side's rows — a tie on part of the data
  isn't a tie;
- a table or column the reconciliation uses has been removed or renamed, a key
  column now holds a different kind of value, or a measure column no longer
  holds numbers or now needs a tolerance it doesn't have;
- a lookup in a side now matches more than one row for some entries;
- neither side has any rows matching its filters, or neither has a value for any
  row;
- the reconciliation hasn't been approved yet;
- it couldn't be run just now, such as when the warehouse is slow to answer.

<Aside type="note">
**Ties out** means the two sides agree row by row, within the tolerance, for the
filters set. It says nothing about whether either side is correct on its own.
</Aside>

## On the Readiness Checklist

A reconciliation linked to a workflow appears on that workflow's item in every
[readiness set](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-readiness/) that includes the
workflow, as the line **Reconciliations tie out**, with each reconciliation's
own sentence and **Open Reconciliation**. A reconciliation that doesn't tie makes
the workflow **Not ready**. One Weave couldn't check, or that hasn't been
approved, makes it **Couldn't check**, unless another line already makes it
**Not ready**.

The checklist runs the approved version of each one, as the person checking it,
so its answer reflects the rows that person can see. It runs up to 25 per
checklist and gives them a minute together; any beyond that say so instead of
running, and count as **Couldn't check**. When Weave checks a set ahead of its
due time, it runs them as the set's owner, and reads tables only if the owner
could when they last saved the set.

## Who Can See What

Anyone who can open the project's Weave tab can list its approved
reconciliations. Running one also needs a role that can read table data. A reader
who isn't an Architect sees each reconciliation's name and side labels, never its
tables, columns or filters, and the reasons Weave gives them name only the
sides.

## Through the API

Reconciliations are available through the platform API:
`GET /analyze/weave/reconciliations` lists a project's reconciliations, and
`GET`, `POST`, `PUT` and `DELETE` on `/analyze/weave/reconciliation` read, create,
replace and delete one. `POST /analyze/weave/reconciliation/approve` approves a
draft: send `digest`, set to the `content_digest` you read with it, which
identifies exactly the version you reviewed. `GET /analyze/weave/reconciliation/run` runs one.

A side can also look up other tables — a join from the side's table to a lookup
table, which must find at most one row for each of the side's rows. Lookups and
the **Weighted average of** measure, which needs a tolerance above 0 like an
average, can be set only through the API; the form keeps them when you edit.

AI agents connected through MCP can find and run reconciliations with
`reconciliation_find` and `reconciliation_run`. An Architect connected with their
own sign-in can also save a draft with `reconciliation_upsert`; an AI acting on
someone's behalf, such as an agent session, Microsoft 365 Copilot or the in-app
chat, can't. No MCP tool approves one.
