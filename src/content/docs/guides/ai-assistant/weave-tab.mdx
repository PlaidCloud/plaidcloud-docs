## Description

Every project has a **Weave** tab. Everything Weave has to say about the project, and
your conversations with it, are in one place, each a tab of its own:

- **Monitor** lists everything Weave has noticed about the project's steps and
  workflows — the problems it found and the things it could not check — in Weave's own
  words. A rail down its left side groups its pages: **Findings**, **Team**,
  **Activity**, **Monthly summary**, **Expected** and **Requests**, then **Readiness** and
  **Reconciliations** under **Close checks**, and **What Weave checks**. From
  **Findings** you can read each finding in full, mark findings as read, open the
  step or workflow a finding is about, and open the AI fix assistant on a problem it
  can repair.
- **Weave AI chat** is in the [Weave dock](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-dock/), on the
  right of every app. Ask multi-step questions about your data, import files, build
  workflow steps and draw diagrams. Conversations persist, so they survive across
  sessions, browsers and devices.

The **Weave** button in the [Weave dock](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-dock/)'s header counts
the problems Weave has told you about that you haven't read yet, across all your projects.

For what Weave checks and what its answers mean, see
[What Weave Checks](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/). To ask the question
finance teams ask on the days that matter — are the workflows a close depends on
ready? — click **Readiness** under **Close checks** in the **Monitor** rail; see
[Check Readiness Before a Close](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-readiness/).

## Open the Weave Tab

There are four ways in:

1. Open the project and select the **Weave** tab, next to **Log**.
2. Click **Open Weave** on the message Weave shows when a workflow run ends. The
   tab opens on **Monitor**, showing the findings about the workflow that ran, or
   all of the project's findings when the message covers several workflows,
   whichever tab was in front and whichever app you were in when the message arrived.
3. Click the **Weave** button in the Weave dock's header and choose a project. See
   [The Weave Button](#the-weave-button).
4. Follow a link to the tab. While the Weave tab is in front, the browser's
   address bar holds a link to it, which you can bookmark or share with someone
   who can open the project. The link opens the tab on **Monitor** for anyone who
   has it, and on the line pointing to the dock for anyone who doesn't.

Everyone who can open the project has the Weave tab. **Monitor** is there for anyone
whose role lets them see the project's steps and workflows; anyone else sees one line,
*Ask Weave in the panel on the right*, with **Open Weave**. Weave reads the whole project the first time
**Monitor** is shown, not when the project opens, so a large project can take a
moment to list.

## The Tabs in the Weave Tab

The Weave tab holds **Monitor** only. Its chat tabs and conversation history moved into
the [Weave dock](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-dock/), where the conversation's title lists your
past conversations and the plus button starts another.

Clicking **Fix** on a finding opens the fix assistant in a window of its own; see
[Fix a Finding with the AI Assistant](#fix-a-finding-with-the-ai-assistant).

## Monitor

The **Monitor** rail lists its pages. **Findings** is the first, and its badge counts the open problems. Click **Refresh** at the top of a page to read Weave's findings again.

### What Findings Shows

Under the **Findings** heading, the two lines at the top are Weave's summary of the
whole project: how many of its steps and workflows Weave checked, what it found,
and what it has no rule for. They carry their own counts, so the findings below
are never mistaken for a verdict on the whole project.

Above the summary, one line gives Weave's verdict, such as *3 open problems, 1 is yours.
Everything else ran clean in the last 24 hours.*, or *Nothing is open.* If Weave couldn't
check something, the line says so rather than calling the project clean. **Show problems**
lists the open problems, and **Show mine** the ones you own.

When you come back after more than 12 hours away, a band says what happened while you were
away, such as *Since you were away (Friday 17:40 UTC): 4 problems were raised, 3 were
resolved and 2 changes were approved.* **Show activity** opens **Activity**, and **Got it**
dismisses the band. After more than 7 days away, it covers the last 7 days.

When a readiness set is coming due, a line gives its state, such as *Month-end close (due
09:00): 11 of 13 ready; 1 not ready; 1 couldn't check.*, with **Open readiness**. A project
with no readiness set, whose workflows run on a monthly schedule, gets a suggested set from
those schedules instead, and a project Architect can click **Make this your month-end set**
to save it. While a set is near its due time, the close card shows what Weave prepared
overnight; see [The Close Prepared Overnight](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-readiness/#the-close-prepared-overnight).

Three tiles sit under the summary: **Open problems**, **Unassigned** and **Oldest open**.

#### Filter the Findings

The buttons under the tiles choose which findings are listed, and each says how
many there are:

- **All** — every finding. This is where **Findings** starts.
- **Open problems** — the problems Weave found.
- **Couldn't check** — the things Weave could not check.
- **Owned by me** — the findings you own, whether someone assigned them to you or
  Weave picked you because you last ran or saved what they're about. Like **All**, it
  includes the ones Weave couldn't check, so it can count more than your row in
  **Team**. See [Give a Finding an Owner](#give-a-finding-an-owner).

The **Owner** and **Workflow** lists next to them narrow the findings further. **Owner** offers **Anyone**, each owner and **Nobody**, and **Workflow** offers **All workflows** and each workflow. When **Findings** shows one workflow's findings, the counts are that workflow's. A
button with nothing under it says so — for example **Nothing listed under
Couldn't check.**

![The Monitor tab's Findings page filtered to Couldn't check, listing one finding that Weave couldn't judge](/images/weave/weave-monitor-couldnt-check.png)

#### Each Finding Is a Row

Findings are grouped by how serious they are: **Problem**, **Needs attention**, **Couldn't
check**, then **Suggestions**, things Weave could do for you that aren't counted as problems.
Within each group, the finding that reaches the most other workflows and dashboards comes
first. A button narrows the list but never reorders it.

A failed workflow run is one row, even when it leaves other workflows and dashboards with
inputs that failed upstream. Those fold under it, and the row says how far it reaches, such
as *Also affects 6 workflows and 3 dashboards, including 2 of yours.* Open the row to see each
one under **Also affected**, with its owner and an **Open** button. The counts, and **Owned by
me**, include what's folded, so a failure that reaches something of yours counts as yours.

![The Monitor tab's Findings page with its rail, three tiles and findings grouped by severity, the first finding open](/images/weave/weave-monitor-findings.png)

Each row shows:

- **The subject** — the step, workflow or table by name, then the rule's title and
  its workflow: **In the workflow** and its name, **In 2 workflows** for a step or table in more
  than one, or **In no workflow**. A finding about a step's run says **From a run
  of the workflow** and the workflow whose run it was. A workflow your role doesn't
  show you appears as **a workflow you can't see**.
- **Owner** — who owns the finding, and why: **ran it last** for whoever last ran
  its workflow, **changed it last** for whoever last saved it, **owns the
  dashboard** for one of a dashboard's owners, or just their name when someone
  assigned it. **Assign** appears when nobody who can see the finding can
  own it. Click the owner to choose who owns the finding. See
  [Give a Finding an Owner](#give-a-finding-an-owner).
- **Open for** — how long the problem has been open, counted from when Weave
  recorded it being raised. Hold the pointer over it for the exact time.
- **One action** — **Fix** where Weave can help repair the problem, otherwise **Open
  step**, **Open workflow**, **Open table**, **Open metric** or **Open dashboard**. The **⋯** menu holds
  the rest: the open action when the row's action is **Fix**, **Assign…**, **Mark read** and **This is expected**.
- **Unread** — a dot until you mark the finding read.

Click a row to open it. An opened finding shows:

- **Weave's sentence** about the step, workflow or table, in full.
- **Why unchecked** — for something Weave could not check, the reason.
- **The evidence** — the facts behind the finding, where they came from and when
  Weave raised it.
- **How Weave knows** — click it to open the check Weave ran and what Weave didn't check. It starts closed. See
  [How Weave Knows](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#how-weave-knows).
- **Also affected** — for a failed run, what it left with failed inputs.
- **Comments** — what people have said about the finding. See
  [Comment on a Finding](#comment-on-a-finding).
- **Fix**, the **Open** action and **Mark read**.

Opening a row marks nothing read.

A large project can have hundreds of findings, so **Findings** lists 20 rows at a
time. Click **Show 20 more findings** at the end of the list for the next ones —
the button counts down when fewer are left.

The reasons a finding can give after **Why unchecked** are:

- **Not fully set up** — the step or workflow is saved but not finished, such as an
  allocation with an empty column mapping or a workflow with no steps yet.
- **Needs run history** — Weave needs runs it has no record of: the workflow hasn't
  run in the last 30 days, hasn't run often enough to compare against, or its runs
  carry no empty-value counts.
- **Needs more context** — Weave lacks something it needs, such as a variable that
  is set differently in each workflow the step belongs to, or run history it could
  not read.
- **Configuration it can't read** — the step's configuration is in a shape this
  check does not read.
- **The check failed** — the rule itself could not run. The finding names the check by its title, such as **Last run failed**.

### Show One Workflow's Findings

Clicking **Open Weave** on the message Weave shows when a workflow run ends brings
**Monitor** to the front, even when a chat was in front, showing that workflow's
findings only, with a line above the list naming the workflow. Weave's findings are
read again when **Monitor** opens this way. A
finding about a table is listed under each workflow that writes the table. Click
**Show all** to see the whole project again.

A workflow with no findings shows **No Weave rule reported this workflow**, and
beneath it what Weave has no rule for across the project. That means no rule
reported the workflow, not that every part of it was checked.

### Mark Findings as Read

- Click **Mark read** on a finding. It loses its **Unread** dot where it is, so
  a **How Weave knows** you have open stays open.
- Click **Mark all read** at the top of **Findings** to mark every finding listed under the
  button you have chosen, including any further down the list than the cards shown
  so far. When **Findings** shows one workflow's findings, only those are marked.

Opening the Weave tab marks nothing read, and neither does being told about a finding
when a run ends. Read state is yours alone.

A finding you have read can come back as unread. Weave treats a finding that
says something new as a new finding — a run finding describes the run it came
from, and a configuration problem that changes is a different problem — so it
appears unread until you read it again. Findings about a workflow with no name or a
duplicated step are identified more precisely than before, so some findings you had
read can show as unread once. If a finding changed between the list
loading and you marking it, **Findings** reloads the list.

Marking a finding read also stops it being mentioned in the message you get when
a run ends, and takes it off the count on the header's **Weave** button. See
[Being Told When a Run Ends](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#being-told-when-a-run-ends).

### Go to the Step, Workflow, Table, Metric or Dashboard

Click **Open step**, **Open workflow**, **Open table**, **Open metric** or
**Open dashboard** on the finding.

- **A workflow** opens on its canvas if it is an Advanced workflow, or on its
  Steps grid otherwise.
- **A step** opens inside its workflow with the step selected. When the step
  belongs to more than one workflow, **Open step** lists them by name under the
  button and you choose which to open — Weave never picks one for you.
- **A step's run finding** opens the workflow whose run it was, since that is the
  run the finding is about.
- **A step that belongs to no workflow you can see** opens on its own.
- **A table** opens in Table Explorer. **Open table** isn't offered if your role
  can't read the project's tables.
- **A metric** opens with its figures calculated for you. See
  [Go To Metric](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-metric-insights/#go-to-metric).
- **A dashboard** opens in a new tab. **Open dashboard** isn't offered if you
  can't open dashboards.

### Fix a Finding with the AI Assistant

Click **Fix** on a finding to open the
[AI fix assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/fixing-workflow-errors/) on it, in a window of
its own. **Fix** is offered only on these findings:

- **Allocation column mapping** and **Step expressions** problems. The assistant starts
  from the problem Weave found in the step's saved configuration.
- **Last run failed**, when one step failed in that run. The assistant opens on that step
  and starts from the error the run recorded. When several steps failed, fix each one
  from the project's **Log** tab.

**Steps that can run before the table they read is written** also offers **Fix** when a
connector is missing, but not for a step the canvas already runs first. This **Fix** isn't
the AI assistant: it opens the workflow's canvas on
[Sync Dependencies](/guides/workflows/sync-dependencies/), where you choose the
connectors to add. It is offered if your role can edit workflows and read the project's
tables, and turning off the fix assistant doesn't hide it.

When the step belongs to more than one workflow, **Fix** lists them by name and you
choose one, as **Open step** does.

The assistant proposes a change and applies it only after you approve it, as it does from
the **Log** tab. See
[How It Works](/guides/ai-assistant/fixing-workflow-errors/#how-it-works).

- If the step has changed since Weave reported the finding, the assistant says the
  finding is no longer current. Click **Refresh** to see what Weave finds now.
- If a failed step has run again since that run, the assistant says the run's error is no
  longer available.
- Each finding has a window of its own, so you can keep several open. Clicking **Fix** on
  a finding whose window is already open brings that window to the front.
- Closing the window reads Weave's findings again, so a problem the fix resolved leaves the
  list.

### Give a Finding an Owner

Every finding Weave reports has an owner, so it reaches the person who can fix it.
Until someone assigns it, the owner is:

- whoever last ran the workflow the finding is about — for a scheduled run,
  whoever last saved its schedule;
- whoever last saved the workflow, if it hasn't run;
- for a step in more than one workflow, or in none, whoever last saved the step;
- for a dashboard, one of its owners: whoever last changed it, if they are an
  owner, or otherwise the first of its owners in order of email address.

A finding about a table that more than one workflow writes has no owner until
someone assigns one.

An owner is always an active member of the project whose role lets them open
what the finding is about; for a dashboard, also someone who can open the
dashboard. For the rows, empty-values and spread-of-values
checks on a project with Row Access declared, it is also someone whose reads
aren't filtered. If the person Weave would pick isn't, the finding says
**No owner**.

To choose the owner, click the owner on the finding (or **Assign**, or **Assign…** in the **⋯** menu) and pick a name. The list shows
only the people who could own the finding. Anyone you assign is told in a
message that the finding is theirs, and it counts on their **Weave** button until
they read it. To hand it back to the owner Weave picks, click **Assign** and then
**Clear Owner**.

The owner you choose stays while the problem does, even when a run gives the
finding new figures. It is cleared once a check run as one of the project's
Architects no longer finds the problem, as in the activity history; if the
problem comes back, it starts again with the owner Weave picks. In a project
where Weave's findings go unread and unchecked for 90 days, owners chosen by hand
go back to the ones Weave picks.

When a workflow run ends, Weave tells each problem's owner about it. See
[Being told when a run ends](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#being-told-when-a-run-ends).
Problems that appear with nothing running are told to their owners by Weave's
hourly check. See
[Being told between runs](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#being-told-between-runs).
To see who owns what across the project, see [See Who Owns What](#see-who-owns-what).

### Comment on a Finding

Open a finding and click **Comments** to read what people have said about the finding and
add your own, such as "Waiting on the SAP extract team". Anyone who can see the
finding can read and add comments.

![An open finding with two comments and the Add comment box](/images/weave/weave-monitor-comments.png)

A comment can't be edited or removed once it's added. Comments are kept in the
project's [Weave activity history](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-activity/), with every
change of owner, and stay with the problem: if it comes back, its earlier comments
come back with it. As with every entry there, PlaidCloud leaves secrets out of a
comment.

Mention a teammate to tell them, or mention @Weave to ask Weave about the finding in the
thread. See [Comments and @Weave](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-team/#comments-and-weave).

### Rate a Finding

Open a finding and, under **Was this worth telling you?**, choose the thumbs-up or the thumbs-down. Not every
finding shows this question. Weave uses the ratings to show, on the **Monthly summary** page,
how useful its findings were; see
[Usefulness](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-monthly-summary/#usefulness).

### Mark a Finding as Expected

Some warnings are normal for your business: a load that always runs light on
Mondays, a table nobody writes in the last week of the quarter. Click
**This is expected** in the finding's **⋯** menu to tell Weave so. Add why in
**Why this is normal (optional)**, and pick a last day in
**Expected until (optional)** if it is only normal for a while. Then click
**Mark as Expected**.

For a run's duration, a step's rows, duration or empty values, and a lookup that adds rows,
choose how much is normal: **This much is normal from now on** marks a range around the
figure Weave judged, such as *41,000 to 47,300 rows*, as usual, and **Only until a day I
pick** marks it for a while. Outside that range, Weave reports the problem again. A figure
from a run on the first or last day of a month is marked as usual only for runs on that
day of a month.

From then on Weave leaves that problem out for everyone in the project: it isn't
listed in **Findings**, isn't counted on the **Weave** button, and isn't told at the end
of a run or by the hourly check. The summary at the top of **Findings** says how many
problems were left out this way, so they are never silently gone, and the step,
workflow or table still counts as checked.

- **This Is Expected** is offered only to the project's Architects, on an unlocked
  project, because it changes what everyone is told.
- It is offered only on warnings that can be normal for a business: a run slower
  than usual, rows, empty values or a spread of values unlike earlier runs, an
  import that found no files, a scheduled run that didn't start or came due during a
  run, and a table not written on time. A failed run, a broken configuration and a
  finding Weave couldn't check are always reported.
- It covers that one problem on that one thing, within its range: one workflow's slow runs, one step's
  rows in one workflow, one column's empty values. Another problem on the same step
  is still reported.
- The day you pick is the last day it applies, in UTC. After it, Weave reports the
  problem again.
- A [readiness check](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-readiness/) still counts a problem
  marked as expected, and says it was marked as expected.

Who marked it, when, why and until when is kept in the project's
[Weave activity history](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-activity/), as is who stopped
expecting it. As with comments, PlaidCloud leaves secrets out of the reason.

### What Weave Has Learned

Click **Expected** in the **Monitor** rail to open **What Weave has learned**: what you told
Weave is normal, and what you told it about this project.

**Usual ranges and expected patterns** lists everything marked as expected in the project.
Each row names the problem and its range, says whether it is **Happening now** or **Not
happening now**, and who marked it as expected, when, why and until when. Above the list,
Weave says how many problems it held back this month because they were marked as expected,
counting each problem once.

**Terms and conventions** lists what people told Weave about the project, such as what a
word means here, each with what it's about, who added it and when. Weave uses these lines
when it answers anyone about the project, in the dock, in chat and in AI tools connected to
PlaidCloud. Type a line in **Add a term or convention for this project** to add one, or use
**Edit** and **Remove** on a line. A line is one short line of plain text, up to 300
characters. Adding, changing or removing one needs a role that can edit the project, and,
for a line about a table, column or analysis path, one that can edit tables. You see only the
lines about things you can open, and every change is kept in the project's activity history.
Weave also offers to remember a line when you correct it in the dock; see
[Teach Weave Your Project's Terms](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-dock/#teach-weave-your-projects-terms).

![The Expected page with one rule marked as expected and its Stop expecting button](/images/weave/weave-monitor-expected.png)

The list shows only what your role lets you open. To have Weave report a problem
again, an Architect clicks **Stop expecting** on its row.

### Automate a Run You Do by Hand

When someone runs a workflow by hand on a pattern, such as on the first working day of each
month around 07:30, Weave notices and lists it under **Suggestions** as **Run by hand on a
pattern**: *Dana has run it by hand on the first working day of each of the last 4 months,
around 07:30.* Weave looks for at least three runs on the same pattern, recent ones, within
two hours of the same time. Runs on a schedule, runs started by another workflow and runs
Weave started don't count, and Weave doesn't suggest a pattern a schedule already covers.

Click **Automate this**. The dock opens on a schedule card with the workflow, the schedule,
when it runs and its next three runs, turned on, with ERP posting off. Approve it to create
the schedule. Schedules start on a whole hour in a time zone, and on a day of the month, so
the card says when what it can write differs from the pattern, such as running on the first of the month
for *the first working day*. A last working day can't be written as a schedule: pick a day
in **Schedules** instead.

### Standing Requests

Click **Requests** in the **Monitor** rail to see the reports, alerts and recipes you asked
Weave to run for you. See
[The Requests Page](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-requests/#the-requests-page).

### See Who Owns What

Click **Team** in the **Monitor** rail to see who owns the project's open
problems. Each row is an owner, with how many problems they own and how long the
oldest has been open, counted from when Weave recorded the problem being raised.
The owner who has waited longest comes first. The problems nobody owns have a row
of their own, **No owner**, so none is missed.

![The Team page listing No owner, Marcus Okafor and Nadia Brennan with their open problems and the oldest's age](/images/weave/weave-monitor-team.png)

- A finding Weave couldn't check isn't counted, just as it isn't on the
  **Weave** button.
- A problem stays open until Weave no longer finds it. Reading it doesn't close it.
- An owner none of whose problems has a recorded raise shows no age.
- **Team** counts the whole project, even when **Findings** shows one workflow's
  findings.
- With no problem open, **Team** says **Nothing listed under Open problems.**, with
  Weave's own summary of how much of the project it checked.

Click a name to see that person's problems in **Findings**, under
**Open problems**, with a line above the list naming them. Click **No owner** to
see the problems nobody owns the same way. Click **Show all** to see the whole
project's problems again, or the **All** chip for every finding. An owner you set
with **Assign** counts straight away, in **Team** and on the **Owned by me** chip,
without the list being read again.

### See What Weave Did

Click **Activity** in the **Monitor** rail to see the project's history of what Weave
noticed, proposed and changed, who approved each change and how it ended, and to
export it as CSV or PDF. Click **Findings** in the rail to come back to the findings. See
[Weave Activity History](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-activity/).

Click **Monthly summary** to see, month by month, how many problems Weave found, how many a
later check no longer found, the changes it made and the problems marked as
expected, with the entries behind each number. See
[Weave Monthly Summary](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-monthly-summary/).

### When Weave Is Unavailable

If Weave's findings can't be read, **Findings** says **Weave is unavailable** instead
of showing an empty list, because an empty list would look like a project with
nothing wrong. Click **Try again**, or **Refresh** at the top of the page.

<Aside type="note">
When Weave has nothing to report, **Findings** shows Weave's own summary of the
project rather than a message saying all is well. That summary says how much of
the project was checked, so it can't be read as covering everything.
</Aside>

Click **What Weave checks** at the bottom of the **Monitor** rail to open
[What Weave Checks](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/).

## The Weave Button

The **Weave** button sits in the header of the [Weave dock](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-dock/),
beside the conversation's title. When Weave has told you about problems
you haven't read yet, in any of your projects, it shows how many beside its icon.
Hover over it to see what the number counts, such as **16 problems Weave told you
about that you haven't read**.

The menu also has **Weave jobs…**, which lists the jobs Weave is working on for you (see
[Jobs That Keep Working](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-dock/#jobs-that-keep-working)),
**Weave email…** and **How Weave uses your data…**. The button shows **…** while a job is
working and nothing is unread. The number on the folded dock includes the same count.

Click the button for a line for each project with unread problems, the project
with the most first, such as **Month End Close: 12 unread problems**. Choose a
line to open that project's Weave tab on **Monitor**, whichever app you are in.
Opening it marks nothing read. A workspace administrator's menu ends with
**Workspace Summary…** and **AI Features…**. **Workspace Summary…** opens every project's monthly Weave numbers; see
[The Workspace Summary](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-monthly-summary/#the-workspace-summary).

The button appears for anyone whose role lets them see a project's steps and
workflows, as **Findings** does.

### What the Count Counts

The count is the problems Weave has told you about in a message that you haven't
read yet in **Findings**. Weave tells you about a problem:

- in the message it shows when a workflow run ends — see
  [Being Told When a Run Ends](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#being-told-when-a-run-ends);
- in the message from its hourly check between runs — see
  [Being Told Between Runs](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-findings/#being-told-between-runs); and
- in the message that tells you someone has assigned a problem to you.

A problem Weave tells you about at the end of every run, such as a workflow that
keeps failing, counts once. Once you have read it, it counts again only when Weave
tells you about it again, as it does when a run finding describes a new run.
Things Weave couldn't check are never counted. A digest and a readiness reminder
don't add to the count. The count is yours alone, like read state.

With nothing to count, the button shows only its icon, and its menu says
**Nothing Weave told you about is unread.** That means you have read everything
Weave has told you about, not that your projects have no problems: a message
covers only some of what Weave finds, and **Findings** lists everything.

### When the Count Changes

The count is read again when you sign in or reload the page, when a Weave message
arrives, when **Findings** lists a project's findings, and when you mark findings
read there. It isn't refreshed on a timer, so findings you mark read in another
browser leave the count here until one of those happens.

Listing a project's findings in **Findings** also brings that project's count up
to date, and so does Weave's hourly check between runs, for each project where it
looks for problems you own. Until one of them does, the count can still include a
problem that has since been fixed, or that has been assigned to someone else. A problem Weave couldn't check
this time stays in the count, since Weave can't tell whether it has been fixed.

A project you can no longer open, or one that has been deleted or is in the
trash, is left out. A problem drops out of the count 90 days after Weave last
told you about it, or 90 days after **Findings** or the hourly check last found it
still a problem and unread, whichever is later.

## Weave AI Chat

### Start a Conversation

1. Open the [Weave dock](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-dock/) on the right of the window, or press **Ctrl+J** (**Cmd+J** on a Mac).
2. Click in the box at the bottom.
3. Type your question and press Enter.

The assistant streams its response back, including any tool calls it made along the way.
Choose **New conversation** in the dock's header to start another conversation.


### Manage Past Conversations

Click the conversation's title in the dock's header to list your conversations in this project, most recent
first. A conversation from before conversations belonged to a project shows
**(no project)** and appears in every project's list. Conversations with the
[fix assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/fixing-workflow-errors/) aren't listed.

**To switch to a past conversation:**

1. Click the conversation in the history list.
2. The full transcript opens in the dock.

**To delete a conversation:**

1. Right-click the conversation in the history list.
2. Select **Delete Thread**, then **Delete**.

<Aside>Deleting the conversation in front of you starts a new one in the dock.</Aside>


### Save a Conversation as PDF

You can export the current chat — your prompts and the assistant's replies, including tables, code blocks, and diagrams — to a PDF.

1. Right-click anywhere in the conversation and choose **Save as PDF**.
2. Your browser's print dialog opens with the conversation laid out as a printable page.
3. Choose **Save as PDF** as the destination and save the file.

<Aside>The option is available once the conversation has at least one exchange. The export uses the responses exactly as rendered on screen, so any diagrams are included as drawn.</Aside>


### Token Usage

Every AI response shows the token usage for that turn — input tokens, output tokens, and a running total for the conversation. Use this to keep an eye on cost as you work.


### Automatic Tool Selection

The assistant decides on its own which tools to call and which documents to consult for each question. There are no toggles to choose what's used; tool selection happens behind the scenes, based on your prompt and the conversation so far.

If the answer doesn't use the tool you expected, rephrase the question or include the table, project, or document name explicitly.


### Business Numbers

In a conversation opened on a project, when you ask for a named business number, such as net revenue or headcount, the assistant is set up to look first for a [metric definition](/integrations/ai-coding-agents/metric-definitions/): a calculation a project Architect has written. If one fits, the figures come from that definition, under your row access, rather than from a query the assistant writes. Words in your question for a breakdown, such as *EMEA*, are matched to the values the metric's dimension holds, and when a word could mean more than one, the assistant asks which you meant. If no metric fits, the assistant queries the tables as usual.


### Figures From a Metric

When an answer uses a metric, its figures appear in a card below the answer, separate from the assistant's text. The card shows the figures as PlaidCloud calculated them, what was asked for (grouping, filters, period, and any comparison, share, ranking or running total) and when they were calculated, and says so when row access limited them.

A card for a metric carries a ✓ and reads **Figures from metric** followed by the metric's name and **(version N)**, such as *Figures from metric Net Revenue (version 3)*. The ✓ means PlaidCloud calculated those figures from the metric's definition. It belongs to the card alone: the assistant's own text is not covered by the ✓, and a number it repeats in its answer never carries one. [How Weave Knows](#how-weave-knows) marks the answer's sentences separately.

A card shows no ✓, and says why, when you reopen a conversation and the metric, or a metric it is built from, has changed since the figures were calculated, is no longer available, or could not be checked.

A card shows up to 100 rows. When there were more, it says how many, and the ✓ covers only the rows it shows. An answer shows up to 10 cards and says how many more it left out. Reopening a conversation shows its cards again, unless you can no longer open the project. The ✓ appears only in the browser; the mobile app and assistants connected over MCP don't show it.


### What a Table, Step or Workflow Does

In a conversation opened on a project, Weave AI can read the [documentation page](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-documentation/) Weave writes for a table, step or workflow — what writes and reads a table, what a step does and which workflows it is in, a workflow's steps in order — and answer from it. The page is written from the project's saved settings when Weave AI asks for it, and follows the same rules for what your role hides.


### Row Access

On a project where an administrator has turned on [Row Access](/administration/access/managing-security-groups-and-assignments/#managing-row-access), the assistant's answers cover only the rows your security group is granted — the same rows you see anywhere else in PlaidCloud.

The assistant writes its own queries, and your grants are applied to each one before it runs. Where a question produces a query the grants cannot be applied to, the assistant tells you so and suggests what to ask for instead, rather than answering from data you are not cleared to see.

When your grants narrow one of those queries, the assistant is told so, and is instructed to treat what it found as your view rather than the whole table. It may say that your view is filtered, but not what is hidden, and it is told not to claim how many rows the table holds or that other values do not exist.


### What Your Role Hides

A table, step, workflow or dimension can be hidden from the Manager or Explorer role. When the assistant reads the project's lists of tables, steps, workflows or dimensions, it sees only the ones your role shows, the same as the project's own lists. It can still work with a table it has just made for you, and still finds a workflow you name exactly when you import into it, rather than telling you either one doesn't exist. If a step your role hides reads or writes a table you ask about, the assistant can tell you a hidden step uses it, but not which step. If your role hides the table itself, the assistant can tell you that steps use the table, but not which ones. Nothing is hidden from an Architect.


### How Weave Knows

Under each answer, **How Weave knows** opens a panel showing what the assistant did
to answer and how much of the answer rests on what it read:

- **What Weave did** — each step it took, such as reading your project's tables and
  columns or running a query.
- **Where it came from** — each query it ran, which only reads data, with a note
  when row-level security limited the rows the query could read, and any table the
  answer names that you can open.
- **Written by AI** — each sentence of the answer, marked:
  - **Matched** — every figure and name in the sentence also appears in what Weave
    read.
  - **Unmatched** — the sentence has a figure or name Weave couldn't find in what it
    read. The AI may have calculated or rounded it, or it may be wrong, so check it
    before you use it.
  - **Not checked** — the sentence has no figures or names to look for.
- **What Weave didn't check** — including a query that failed, or an answer given
  without reading your project at all.

Weave marks the sentences by looking for each figure and name in
the results the assistant's tools returned. A figure you typed in your question,
or one the query itself contains, doesn't count as evidence. **Matched** is not a
guarantee: Weave doesn't check that each figure is attached to the right name,
whether it rose or fell, or that a query asked the right question, and a common
figure can match by chance. A figure with a unit Weave can't read, such as `10.5ms`,
leaves its sentence **Unmatched**.

The panel is kept with the conversation, so it's still there when you reopen it.
A fix-assistant reply taken from PlaidCloud's catalog of known errors says so.

### Multi-Step Requests

You don't have to split a task into separate messages. Ask for the whole thing at once and the assistant works through the steps in order, carrying the result of each into the next:

- "Find a table with cost data, then show me a few rows from it" — locates the table, then queries it
- "Which tables have margin data? Show me the rows where margin is negative" — finds the table, then filters it
- "Import the sales file from the Demo Data account and chart the monthly totals" — imports the file, then draws the chart

The assistant also remembers the conversation, so you can follow up without repeating yourself. After it shows a table, "now show only the negative rows" or "draw that as a chart" continues from what's already there.

Before it makes a change, the assistant tells you what it's about to do and waits for your go-ahead rather than guessing. That covers importing a file, building a step, creating a project, workflow or dimension, copying a table, changing a table's properties, adding or removing labels, and destructive operations such as dropping or deleting data. Nothing is written until you approve. An import or export card names the table, document account and workflow it uses, and the assistant won't offer one naming something that doesn't exist or that you can't open.

Your approval counts only in your very next reply. If you change the request instead, the assistant shows you the new version to approve rather than building the earlier one. Proposals name tables the way you do, never by internal id, and names with underscores, such as `agg_freight_by_country`, appear exactly as written in every AI chat, the expression assistant included.


### Importing a File

The assistant can load a file from one of your project's document accounts into a new table, without building a workflow step. Tell it the account, the path, and a name for the new table:

- "Import /imports/customers.csv from the Demo Data account into a new table called Customers"

You can name **Project Default** as the account. The assistant reads from whichever account the project has set as its default, and an import or export step it builds keeps following that default if it changes. If the project has no default set, the assistant tells you so.

It first inspects the file and shows you the columns it detected, then waits for you to confirm before importing — your chance to catch a wrong file or an unexpected schema. Confirm, and it loads the table and reports the new table's name, column count, and row count.

Nothing is created until you reply, and what runs is the plan you were shown rather than whatever was re-sent with your approval. If the assistant cannot pin something down — two tables share a name, or the account you gave does not exist — it asks instead of proposing a guess, so there is nothing to approve until it has an answer.

<Aside>For a re-runnable import that lives in a workflow so it can be executed again later, ask the assistant to "create an import step" in a named workflow instead — it builds the step rather than doing a one-shot load. Ask it to "create an export step" to write a table out to a document account the same way. Both follow the same approve-first flow, and both show you the workflow they would attach to — including one they would create for you — before anything exists.</Aside>


### Building a Workflow Step

The assistant can build a step that reads one table — or looks each of its rows up in a second one — and writes the columns you want into another table. Say what you want in plain language:

- "Add a step that totals claim amounts and claim counts by region for 2024, reading from Claims Cost by Line Region into Claims by Region"
- "Build a step that filters the claims table to 2025 and keeps only the line, region, and amount columns"
- "Create a step that looks each claim up in Rates on claim id and writes the rate alongside the amount"
- "Add a column that multiplies amount by rate, stored as a number"

Between them these cover keeping, renaming, dropping and computing columns, writing a fixed value, setting a column's type, filtering rows, grouping with totals, and looking each row up in a second table. Name your tables however you normally say them — the assistant matches on the name you use, and asks which you meant if two tables share one.

It always proposes before it builds, and **nothing is created until you reply**. The summary is the whole of what you are agreeing to, so it spells out every choice the step makes: the tables it reads, each column it writes and — where that column isn't a straight copy — what it was renamed from, which table it was taken from, what it computes, what fixed value it holds; the filter on the rows going in and any filter on the rows coming out; whether the step groups and what it computes for each group; the table it writes into; the folder it files the step under; and whether it joins a workflow. If the proposal isn't what you meant, say what to change and it proposes again — and if anything it described has moved in the meantime, it asks you to approve the new version rather than building the old one.

A column the step computes, and one you give a fixed value to, are **stored as text unless you say otherwise**. Text doesn't fail when you calculate with it — it sorts 9 above 10 and totals nothing — so the summary says which columns have no type set, and you can ask for one.

#### What a Lookup Does to Your Rows

When the step looks rows up in a second table, the summary also says what it matches on and what the match does to your rows:

- **The match itself drops nothing.** Every row of the first table carries through it whether or not it found a match, with columns from the second table filled in where there is one and left blank where there isn't. A filter or a grouping can still change what comes out — that is what the rest of this list is about.
- **A lookup never multiplies your rows.** The second table is collapsed to one row per matching key before the join, so a key it holds twice cannot turn one of your rows into two.
- **Where the second table does hold a key more than once**, each column is filled in on its own, taking the smallest of the matching values for that column. The values you get can therefore be a combination that appears in no single row of that table. The summary says how many keys this affects and how many there are, so you can see whether it arises in your data at all — and says so plainly when it could not check.
- **A filter runs after the lookup.** A row that found no match has its second-table columns blank, so whether such rows survive depends on how your filter treats a blank.
- **If the step also groups**, the output is one row per group rather than one per row you started with. Where you group on a column that came from the second table, the rows that found no match are grouped under a blank value for it — a blank groups like any other value rather than being skipped.

Ask for everything from both tables and the step would write two columns under the same name, which it cannot do; name the columns you want instead, saying which table each comes from where both carry the name.

The step can write into an existing table or a new one. When the table is new, the proposal says so and says where it will go, and the table and the step are created together only after you approve; if you don't approve, neither exists. The table is **rebuilt from scratch every time the step runs** — whatever is in it beforehand is discarded, so point the step at a table that holds only its output rather than one holding data you need. The summary says this too, naming the table it will replace. New steps are left unattached unless you name a workflow, and attaching to one is part of what you approve rather than something added afterwards.

<Aside>Loading a file into a table, or writing a table out to one, are different steps — ask for an "import step" or an "export step" instead.</Aside>


### Diagrams

The assistant can draw a simple diagram to explain a process, a data flow, or how things relate — handy when a picture is clearer than a paragraph. Ask in plain language:

- "Draw a flowchart of an ETL: extract, transform, load"
- "Diagram how an order, a customer, and a line item relate"
- "Show the approval steps as a sequence diagram"

The diagram renders inline in the response as a picture, with a short plain-language description alongside it. The assistant draws a wide range of types and picks whichever fits the request — flowcharts, sequence and state diagrams, entity-relationship and class diagrams, pie charts, bar and trend charts, Sankey flows, quadrant charts, Gantt and timeline schedules, user journeys, mind maps, and more. The assistant won't restyle a diagram (custom colours, fonts, themes, or branding) — for styled, shareable visuals, build a [Dashboard](/guides/dashboards/) instead.

<Aside type="caution">The diagram assistant has no tools and cannot read your project's tables or data — it draws only from what you describe in the chat. To diagram real tables or lineage, describe them in your message, or ask a discovery question first and then say "draw that as a diagram" (the conversation carries over).</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Diagrams are drawn from figures you supplied">
A diagram that carries quantities — a Sankey flow, a pie chart, a bar or trend chart — needs a number for every band or bar. Where the assistant hasn't been given one, it now asks you for it rather than drawing a plausible-looking figure, so a picture that looks authoritative is not quietly made up. If you want a chart of real data, ask the discovery question first ("what were the monthly totals?") and then say "draw that as a chart", so the figures it draws are the ones it just showed you.
</Aside>

Every diagram arrives as a picture. If one occasionally came back as a block of diagram source code rather than an image, that no longer happens — whichever notation the assistant chooses, the response renders it.

If a diagram genuinely can't be drawn, the response shows the underlying diagram text along with a brief "Couldn't render this diagram." note — rephrase or simplify your request and ask again.


## Expression AI

Weave AI also works outside the Weave tab. The Expression Editor — used by Project Table, Calculate, Filter, and any other step that takes expressions — has it built in as a side panel.

1. Open a workflow step that uses expressions
2. Open the editor for the column you want to fill in
3. The AI panel sits alongside the expression editor; ask it to draft, explain, or fix the expression
4. Hover a suggested expression and open its menu to insert it at the cursor, append it, replace the editor's contents, or copy it

The chat already has the column list and types from the current step, and the expression as it stands in the editor, so you can ask questions like "concatenate first_name and last_name with a space" or "fix this expression" without restating the schema or pasting the expression. Each message carries the editor's text as it is at that moment, so after an edit, a follow-up question is about the edited version.

It also knows which lakehouse your project runs on. It suggests and checks the functions that lakehouse provides, and links to that lakehouse's function reference, so the same request can give a different expression on a Databend project and a StarRocks one. Expression checks are available for Databend and StarRocks projects.

Every expression the assistant gives you is checked before you see it. If one breaks a rule the checker can judge from the expression alone — for example, a safe-divide guard such as `div0` with a literal zero where the divisor belongs — the assistant withholds it and shows **Expression withheld.** with the reason in its place. Ask it to try again and it starts from that reason. The check does not judge column names, so a misspelled column is not caught here.
