## Description

Weave works beside you in the workflow editor and the step forms. It suggests, counts and
checks, but it never saves anything: a suggestion is yours only once you keep it and save
the step, and a check never stops you saving.

## Suggestions in a Step Form

Once you've started filling in a step form, click into an empty field. Where Weave has a
suggestion, it appears faintly under the field, with a line saying where it came from and
a **Use** button.

- Press **Tab** or click **Use** to keep it.
- Press **Escape**, or keep typing, to dismiss it.

Weave suggests:

- the key columns of a join, such as `cost_center = cost_center`;
- the column mapping of a new step;
- the **WHERE** filter;
- an empty expression cell, and an empty expression editor.

The line under a suggestion says where it came from, such as *Used in 3 other steps that
read Freight*, *The columns of Freight*, or for a join, *These keys come from another join
step in this project that joins the same two tables.* When the keys only have similar
names, it says *These columns have similar names in both tables. Check them before you
save.*

Weave suggests only from steps you can see, and leaves out anything that would stop the
step saving. A field that already has a value, and a read-only form, get no suggestion.
The column mapping is suggested on new steps only.

**Suggest settings**, in a step's right-click menu, the command palette and the dock's `/`
list, asks for every suggestion on the step at once.

## What Comes Next

After you add a step to the canvas, a **What next?** button appears to its right. Click it
for up to three tiles, each a kind of step, such as **Summarize**, **Look up** or
**Export: CSV**. A tile opens that step's form, filled in and not yet saved.

Weave suggests the steps that most often come next in this project. A project with little
history gets common next steps instead. **What comes next?** in the right-click menu,
command palette and dock does the same.

## Count the Rows Before You Save

The strip above **Save** in a step form says how the step last ran, such as *Last run
46,800 rows, ended 11 Nov 06:12 UTC*, or **Not run yet**.

Click **Count rows** to count what the step would write with the settings in front of
you, without saving or running it:

> This change: 46,912 rows (last run 46,800; usually 45,100 to 47,300, compared with the
> last 8 runs)

Click **Show the rows** for the first 20 rows. A line under the count says how far the
step reaches: click **Show the steps** or **Show the dashboards** to see what reads its
output.

- Counting works for Extract, Lookup, Join (inner, outer and anti), Union and Union All
  steps. Other steps say **No preview for this step type yet**.
- A step that can't be counted says why, such as a table it reads that hasn't been built
  yet.
- The count stops after 5 seconds.
- If row access limits what you can see, the count is of the rows you can see, and says so.

**Preview** in a step's right-click menu, the command palette or the dock gives the same
last-run line and count.

## See a Step's Data on the Canvas

Rest the pointer on a step that writes a table. A small window shows the table, its rows
and when the step last ran, with:

- **Show the first rows** — reads the first 10 rows, as you, and keeps them with the time
  they were read.
- **Open table** — opens the table in Table Explorer.
- **Ask Weave** — opens the dock on the table.

Anyone who can read the project's tables sees this, including members who can only view
the workflow.

## Checked When You Run

When you run a workflow, with **Run Workflow**, a range of steps or **Run Selected**, Weave
checks its settings and structure, the same checks as **Check** makes on them. If all is
well, you see nothing. If something is wrong, a strip above the canvas says *Weave found 2
problems in the workflow "Month-End Close"*, followed by the first problem:

- **Review** opens the full result in the Weave Check window.
- **Stop run** stops the run. It appears while the run is going, when a problem is an
  error rather than a warning.

Each step with a problem gets a mark on the canvas, which names the problem when you point at it.
Weave checks again only after you've saved a change to a step or to the workflow, and
raises the strip only for problems it hasn't already shown you. Lineage, warehouse
columns and row-level security are still checked when you click **Check**. Point at
**Check** to see when the settings were last checked.

## When a Step Fails

A step whose last run failed has a red dot on the canvas: *Last run failed. Select it to
see why.* Select it, and the **Last Run Error** card in the Inspector has a button:

- **Fix**, when Weave can propose a fix. See
  [Fixing Workflow Errors with AI](/guides/ai-assistant/fixing-workflow-errors/).
- **Ask Weave** otherwise, and on a warning. Weave explains the error, and answers the
  errors it knows from PlaidCloud's catalog of known errors.

The **⋯** beside it holds the other Weave actions. Error messages elsewhere offer
**Ask Weave** too: the **Action Failed** dialog, a step that couldn't be saved, a workflow
that couldn't start, and a CSV import that failed.

## Weave Working on a Workflow

While Weave runs a job on a workflow, such as a build or a fix, its icon joins the people
viewing the workflow on the canvas. Point at it to see *Weave, working for* and the name of
the person it's working for.

The canvas toolbar's comments button opens the workflow's comments. See
[Comments and @Weave](/guides/ai-assistant/weave-team/#comments-and-weave).

<Aside type="note">
Suggestions, counts, the canvas window and the check on run read your project as you, and
change nothing.
</Aside>
