Once an allocation has run, you can ask the [AI Assistant](/guides/ai-assistant/using-ai-assistant/) about its results in plain English — instead of building pivot tables by hand. The assistant reads your allocation model directly, so it already knows how each cost line is built and what drives it.

Open the project's **AI** tab and ask your question. The assistant picks the right analysis automatically; you don't choose a tool.

## What You Can Ask

### Why an Allocation Changed

The most common question — compare two periods and explain the movement:

> Why did the revenue-allocated cost line change from January to February?

The assistant returns:

- The **size** of the change — before, after, and the delta.
- **What drove it** — whether the input cost pool grew or shrank, or the driver mix shifted.
- The **top contributors** — the accounts, cost centres, or products that moved the most.

### Drill Into One Slice

Narrow the same question to a single member to get a precise breakdown:

> Explain just the Operations cost centre.

The assistant separates the two effects — how much came from the **overall pool** moving versus the slice's **share** of it changing. For example: Operations fell because the pool shrank, even though Operations took a larger share of it.

### Where Values Come From, and What They Feed

Trace an allocation's lineage in either direction:

> Where does this cost line come from?
>
> What does the GL cost pool feed downstream?

The assistant lists the upstream sources and drivers, or **every** downstream step the table feeds — across allocations and the transforms between them.

### What Drives an Allocation

> Is revenue used as a driver, and where?

The assistant lists each step that uses the table as a driver — the basis for the split — versus an input, the values being spread.

### What a Change Would Do

Ask a forward-looking question:

> If revenue rises by 1 million next quarter, which cost lines are affected?

The assistant names the affected outputs and an estimated size for each.

<Aside type="note">
What-if estimates are an **upper bound** based on your current allocation configuration — they show the most a target could move, and the values are not additive across targets. Use them to frame which outputs are in scope, not as a precise forecast.
</Aside>

### What Allocations Exist

> What cost allocations are in this project?

The assistant lists the allocation models and their final output tables — useful for orienting yourself in an unfamiliar model.

## Understanding the Answer

Each explanation comes with a quality signal:

- **Clean** — the change was fully attributed to its drivers. This includes **filtered** allocations, those scoped to specific accounts: the filter is applied automatically, so the contributors are exactly the rows in scope.
- **Partial** — the assistant can show *what* changed and *where* — the totals and top contributors are correct — but cannot fully attribute the *why* on its own. It tells you why, and what to ask next.

When a result is partial, the guidance usually points you to the **slice drill-down** above, which gives a real decomposition for a single member.

## Current Limitations

<Aside type="caution" title="Dimension allocations">
For allocations driven by a **dimension hierarchy** — for example, splitting a cost centre across a reporting structure — the assistant reports the correct totals and per-member movements but flags the precise cause as partial. Attributing it needs point-in-time hierarchy history, which is a later release. The slice drill-down gives a usable breakdown in the meantime.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Cascaded allocations">
When one allocation feeds another — a cascade — a project-wide "why" would mix the wrong rows, so the assistant asks you to scope the question to a slice, such as a specific region, product, or cost centre, and then explains it.
</Aside>

## Tips

- **Name the table and the period.** "Why did `cost_line_rev` change from 2025-01 to 2025-02?" gets a sharper answer than "why did costs change?".
- **Pick a real before and after period** with a movement you can sanity-check.
- If the assistant doesn't analyze the table you meant, say its name explicitly — see [Automatic Tool Selection](/guides/ai-assistant/using-ai-assistant/#automatic-tool-selection).
